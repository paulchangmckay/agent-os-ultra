# Cheatsheet

## The One-Line Generator
**Everything gains or loses from volatility. Fragility is what loses from volatility and uncertainty.** Before analyzing anything else in this book, apply this test first — it resolves almost every case.

## Classification Decision Rule
| Question | Fragile (concave) | Robust | Antifragile (convex) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Does harm/benefit accelerate at the extremes? | Harm accelerates | Flat response | Benefit accelerates |
| Does it like volatility? | No — hurt by it | Indifferent | Yes — needs it |
| One large shock vs. many small equivalent shocks? | Large shock hurts far more | Same either way | Large positive shock helps far more |
| Effect of removing all stressors? | Improves it (short-term) | No change | Degrades it (undercompensation) |

## Decision Rules ("When X, do Y, because Z")
- **When you can't estimate tail risk (almost always)** → use a barbell (90% safe / 10% speculative, or similar split), **because** it caps ruin without requiring accurate risk estimation.
- **When a system has gone a long time without a setback** → treat that as a warning sign (accumulating "weak hands"), not reassurance, **because** suppressed volatility resurfaces as a larger shock later.
- **When someone offers a forecast or policy opinion** → check their personal downside exposure first, **because** an agent with no skin in the game is under no discipline to be right.
- **When a patient/system is near-normal/healthy** → default to non-intervention, **because** benefit from treatment is convex to severity while iatrogenic risk stays roughly flat — the math favors leaving mild cases alone.
- **When a patient/system is severely compromised** → intervene aggressively, **because** the same convexity now favors action; the risk-of-inaction exceeds the risk-of-treatment.
- **When judging whether a nonperishable thing (technology, book, institution) will persist** → check how long it's already survived, **because** the Lindy effect makes past longevity predictive of future longevity (opposite of aging organisms).
- **When forecasting "the future"** → subtract what's currently fragile rather than add imagined novelties, **because** via negativa is the only forecasting method with a defensible track record.
- **When someone can't define or theorize their own successful practice** → don't discount their competence, **because** tacit/practiced knowledge is frequently more reliable than articulated theory (green lumber, Fat Tony vs. Socrates).
- **When a "free" health/performance benefit for the healthy appears with no downside** → be suspicious, **because** nature's long selection process makes true free lunches rare (steroids, trans fat).
- **When professional income depends on a policy position's outcome** → discount that person's opinion on the policy, **because** profession shapes belief more reliably than belief shapes profession.

## Trade-off Matrix: Barbell vs. Medium-Risk Strategy
| Dimension | Medium-risk ("moderate") | Barbell (safe + speculative) |
|---|---|---|
| Risk-of-ruin | Nonzero, hard to estimate | Capped and known |
| Tail-risk estimation required | Yes (and usually wrong) | No |
| "Efficiency" appearance | Looks smoother | Looks less efficient on average |
| Behavior under Black Swan | Can be wiped out | Downside capped, upside open |
| Taleb's verdict | "Sucker's game" | Structurally sound |

## Thresholds & Defaults
- **Hypertension treatment benefit**: mild ~5.6% benefit (1 in 18) vs. severe ~72% benefit (2 in 3) — same drug, same iatrogenic risk, wildly different net benefit by severity. Default: treat severe, leave mild alone.
- **Extreme-event rarity**: ~10,000x more 0.1%-magnitude market events than 10%-magnitude ones; ~3 million harmless microearthquakes/year vs. rare newsworthy magnitude-6+ events. Default: assume the tail is far rarer and far more consequential than intuition suggests.
- **Lindy default**: a nonperishable thing that has survived N years can be expected to survive roughly another N years (rule of thumb, not exact) — the older it is, the longer its expected remaining life.
- **90/10 barbell default**: 90% capital-preservation / 10% max-speculative is Taleb's illustrative split — not a universal ratio, but the *shape* (safe majority + small bounded-loss speculative minority, nothing in the middle) is the actual rule.

## Tells & Smells (Fast Heuristics)
- **"It's perfectly legal"** offered in response to an ethics question → tell for the legal/ethical gap being actively exploited (the Alan Blinder problem).
- **A system running near maximum capacity, praised as "efficient"** → tell for hidden fragility to small increases in load (traffic, airports, tightly-coupled supply chains).
- **A forecaster/pundit with no personal downside** → tell for an agency problem; treat their confidence as unearned.
- **"We tend to live longer now" used to defend medicine broadly** → tell for conflating convex-case (severe illness) medical benefit with concave-case (marginal/elective) medical harm.
- **A claimed unconditional health/performance benefit with no downside** → tell for a "sucker's trade" — check for a hidden, delayed cost.
- **Someone dismisses a simple, correct idea as "too trivial"** → tell for complexity bias (mistaking mathematical/rhetorical dressing for rigor).
- **"Do you have evidence this is harmful?" used to defend a new, unnatural intervention** → tell for the burden-of-proof fallacy; the non-natural should have to prove safety, not vice versa.
- **A long calm period in a market/organization with no correction** → tell for accumulating "weak hands" and hidden fragility, not health.
- **Marketing that calls something "healthy," "social," or "knowledge-based"** → tell for likely being the opposite (Taleb's branding-inversion rule).
