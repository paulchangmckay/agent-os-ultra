# Glossary

**Agency problem** — When one party (the agent: doctor, broker, politician, regulator) has interests divorced from the person they serve (the principal), and gives advice/action geared to benefit themselves. (Ch 7, Ch 23)

**Antifragile** — The property of gaining from disorder, volatility, and stressors, not merely enduring them; fragility with a negative sign in front of it. The true opposite of fragile (not "robust"). (Ch 1)

**Antifragile tinkering / Bricolage** — A class of trial and error in which small errors are "the right kind of mistakes," equivalent to the rational flâneur. (Ch 13)

**Barbell (bimodal) strategy** — A dual strategy combining two extremes (maximum safety, maximum speculation) while avoiding the "golden middle"; reduces risk of ruin while retaining upside. (Ch 11)

**Black Swan** — A large-impact, hard-to-predict, rare event, especially one that dominates outcomes in Extremistan. (referenced throughout; developed in Taleb's prior book, applied here to fragility)

**Causal opacity** — The inability to trace cause to effect in a complex, interdependent system, making conventional cause-based analysis unreliable. (Ch 3)

**Convex / Convexity effect (positive)** — A payoff curve that bends outward like a smile: more upside than downside from a given deviation; likes volatility. Technical name for antifragility. (Ch 18)

**Concave / Convexity effect (negative)** — A payoff curve that bends inward like a frown: more downside than upside; disklikes volatility. Technical name for fragility. (Ch 18)

**Convex transformation** — The general method of reshaping one's *exposure* to an unknown variable x (via a barbell or similar structure) rather than trying to understand or predict x itself. (Ch 25)

**Domain dependence** — The failure to transfer a recognized principle from one context/domain to another that has the same underlying structure. (Ch 1)

**Epiphenomenon** — A correlated-but-non-causal association mistaken for causation (e.g., "rich countries are educated" mistaken for "education causes wealth"). (Ch 14–15)

**Extremistan** — A random-variable environment where a single observation can dominate the total (e.g., a writer's income); fat-tailed, power-law distributed. Contrast Mediocristan. (Ch 5, Ch 8)

**Fat Tony** — Taleb's streetwise character archetype: detects fragility by instinct/exposure rather than prediction, insists on skin in the game, distrusts definitional/verbal knowledge. (Ch 9, 17, 23–24)

**Fragile** — Harmed by disorder, volatility, and time; more downside than upside from a source of variation (unfavorable asymmetry). (Ch 1, Ch 10)

**Fragility Transfer Theorem** — The formal link: convex exposure (over some range) ↔ likes volatility (up to a point); concave exposure ↔ dislikes volatility. (Ch 18, Appendix)

**Fragilista** — Someone with an illusion of understanding complex systems, who builds/defends fragile structures while mistaking what they don't understand for nonsense. (Ch 9, throughout)

**Green Lumber Fallacy** — Mistaking one kind of necessary knowledge (the visible, narrated kind) for another, less visible but actually load-bearing kind; named for a top commodities trader who didn't know "green" meant freshly cut, not painted green. (Ch 14)

**Hormesis** — A small dose of a harmful substance or stressor that actively benefits the organism (medicine, not just protection); the beneficial dose-response nonlinearity. (Ch 1)

**Iatrogenics** — Net harm caused by the healer/intervener, in excess of any benefit; from Greek *iatros*, healer. (Ch 7)

**Lecturing Birds on How to Fly** — Taleb's metaphor for theory/academia claiming credit for practices that practitioners developed first through tinkering. (Ch 13, Ch 15)

**Lindy Effect** — For nonperishable/informational things, every additional day of survival *increases* expected remaining life expectancy; for perishable things (organisms), it decreases it. (Ch 20)

**Ludic vs. ecological** — Ludic: a domain with fixed, explicit, game-like rules (classroom, chess). Ecological: real life, with no fixed rules, where skills don't transfer reliably from the ludic. (Ch 16)

**Mediocristan** — A random-variable environment dominated by the mediocre; no single observation can meaningfully affect the aggregate (e.g., a dentist's income); thin-tailed, Gaussian. Contrast Extremistan. (Ch 5, Ch 8)

**Mithridatization** — Building immunity via repeated small-dose exposure to a toxin; a robustness-level (not yet antifragile) precursor mechanism. (Ch 1)

**Naive interventionism** — The urge to "do something" without weighing probabilistic benefit against probabilistic (including hidden/delayed) harm. (Ch 7)

**Neomania** — A love of change/novelty for its own sake; forecasts the future by adding rather than subtracting; incompatible with the Lindy effect. (Ch 20)

**Nonlinearity** — A response that isn't a straight line: doubling the input produces more or less than double the effect. The mathematical basis of both fragility (concave) and antifragility (convex). (Ch 18)

**Opacity** — The condition of not being able to see the true risk in a situation (e.g., not seeing the barrel in Russian roulette), leading to illusions of understanding. (Ch 21, glossary)

**Optionality** — An asymmetric payoff structure: "the right, but not the obligation," where a small bounded cost buys exposure to a large potential gain. (Ch 12)

**Philosopher's Stone / Convexity bias** — The exact measure of benefit derived from nonlinearity or optionality; the gain from variable-intensity exposure over steady exposure at the same average. (Ch 19, glossary)

**Redundancy** — Seemingly wasteful extra capacity (extra kidneys, extra cash) that is actually investment against uncertainty, not mere insurance. (Ch 2)

**Skin in the game** — Bearing real personal exposure to the consequences of one's opinions, forecasts, or professional actions. (Ch 23)

**Sucker/nonsucker** — Taleb's practical-ethics sorting: suckers are exposed to fragility they don't understand; nonsuckers see and exploit it. (Ch 9)

**Triad, the** — Taleb's core three-way classification: Fragile / Robust / Antifragile, replacing predictive methods as the basis for decision-making. (Ch 8, throughout)

**Turkey problem** — Mistaking a long run of calm/safety for permanent safety, unaware of the Black Swan (Thanksgiving) that ends it; also mistaking absence of evidence for evidence of absence. (Ch 8)

**Via negativa** — Forecasting or acting by *removal/subtraction* (of the fragile, the unnatural, the unnecessary) rather than by addition; the only rigorous method under real uncertainty. (Ch 20, Ch 22)
