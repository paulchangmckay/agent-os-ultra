# Patterns & Techniques

## Barbell Strategy
**When to use**: Any time uncertainty is high and tail risk can't be reliably estimated (almost always).
**How**: Put the large majority of an exposure in a maximally safe, boring position (known, bounded max loss) and a small remainder in maximally aggressive, high-upside bets (small known max loss, large/unbounded potential gain). Nothing in the "medium risk" middle.
**Trade-offs**: Sacrifices smooth, "efficient"-looking average returns for a hard cap on ruin risk. Requires discipline to keep the two halves separate rather than blending into a mediocre middle.

## Seneca's Mental Write-Off (Premeditated Worst Case)
**When to use**: Before any risky undertaking, trade, or period of success/wealth.
**How**: Explicitly imagine the worst realistic outcome has already happened before starting; treat the rest of the day/venture as a bonus. Applied literally by Taleb (resignation letter written and locked away before starting a new job).
**Trade-offs**: Requires real discipline to maintain during good times, when it's least intuitive but most needed. Not a substitute for actually bounding real downside.

## Fragility Detection Heuristic (Acceleration of Harm)
**When to use**: To assess whether an entity (company, system, model) is headed for eventual collapse, without needing to predict the trigger.
**How**: Check whether harm/loss accelerates as a variable deviates further from normal — plot or estimate the response curve; if it bends toward faster-growing harm at the extremes (concave), it's fragile. Applied to Fannie Mae's exposure in 2003, five years before its 2008 collapse.
**Trade-offs**: Tells you fragility exists and roughly how much, but not when the triggering event will occur — patience is required, and being early looks like being wrong.

## Via Negativa (Subtractive Prophecy / Subtractive Health)
**When to use**: Forecasting the future, or improving health/well-being/any complex system.
**How**: Identify what is currently fragile and remove/subtract it, rather than imagining and adding speculative new elements. In health: remove unnatural additions (processed foods, unnecessary medication, chronic modern stressors) before adding new interventions.
**Trade-offs**: Feels less proactive/impressive than adding solutions ("look what I avoided" gets no reward compared to "look what I did") — expect this approach to be systematically under-rewarded by incentive structures.

## Optionality-Seeking (Thales' Option Structure)
**When to use**: Structuring any decision, contract, investment, or creative bet.
**How**: Look for "the right, but not the obligation" — pay a small, bounded cost for exposure to a large, uncertain upside. Don't require yourself to predict the outcome; the payoff structure does the work.
**Trade-offs**: Options that are known to be options (financial options with visible sellers) can be priced expensively; the best optionality is often free or hidden (unsolicited invitations, embedded contract terms) and requires noticing it rather than paying for it.

## Skin-in-the-Game Filter
**When to use**: Evaluating any forecast, policy recommendation, or expert opinion before acting on it.
**How**: Ask what the source personally stands to lose if they're wrong. Discount opinions with no matching downside exposure; weight opinions backed by real personal risk more heavily.
**Trade-offs**: Can be hard to verify actual exposure (hidden hedges, indirect incentives); doesn't guarantee correctness, only reduces a specific, common source of unreliability (the agency problem).

## Severity-Gated Intervention (Medical/Policy Convexity Rule)
**When to use**: Deciding whether to intervene medically, regulatorily, or organizationally.
**How**: Check where the entity sits on the severity spectrum. Near-normal/healthy → default to non-intervention (let the natural/default process run). Severely compromised → intervene aggressively, "no holds barred." Benefit from intervention is convex to severity; iatrogenic risk is roughly flat.
**Trade-offs**: Requires resisting the "do something" instinct for marginal cases, which is emotionally and institutionally difficult (under-rewarded, as in via negativa).

## Green Lumber Check
**When to use**: Assessing whether someone's stated theory/credentials actually match what drives their real-world success.
**How**: Verify that the "knowledge" assumed necessary from the outside (credentials, correct terminology, textbook understanding) is actually load-bearing for the outcome — or whether success comes from a different, less visible, non-narratable kind of knowledge (market-selected practice, tacit skill).
**Trade-offs**: Can lead to under-valuing genuine formal expertise in domains where it *is* load-bearing (physics, structural engineering) — apply domain-by-domain, not universally.

## Lindy Check (Longevity as Evidence)
**When to use**: Judging whether a nonperishable thing (technology, book, idea, institution) will persist.
**How**: Ask how long it has already survived. For nonperishable/informational things, longer past survival predicts longer future survival — don't assume age implies imminent obsolescence.
**Trade-offs**: Doesn't apply to perishable things (organisms, single physical objects), where the opposite intuition (aging reduces remaining life) is correct — classify first.

## Detouristification / Decommoditized Exploration
**When to use**: Personal education, travel, reading, or any curiosity-driven pursuit.
**How**: Resist pre-packaged, fully scheduled experiences; inject randomness (don't decide the next travel destination until you're mid-trip; switch books the moment you're bored rather than quitting or persisting through boredom).
**Trade-offs**: Less efficient-looking and harder to measure than structured curricula/itineraries; requires tolerance for aimlessness and self-directed judgment.
