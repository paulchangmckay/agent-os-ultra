# Chapter IV: A Few Matters of Form

## Core Idea
A short reference chapter of manuscript-formatting conventions — headings, numerals, parentheses, quotations, references, syllable division, and titles — covering the mechanical presentation choices that surround good sentences but aren't sentence-level rules themselves.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Parenthetical punctuation rule**: a sentence containing a parenthetical expression is punctuated outside the parentheses exactly as if the expression were absent; inside, it's punctuated as if it stood alone, except the final stop is dropped unless it's a question mark or exclamation point.
  - How: "I went to his house yesterday (my third attempt to see him), but he had left town" — the comma after the parenthesis belongs to the outer sentence, not the inner aside.
- **Quotation introduction rule**: formal/documentary quotations take a colon; quotations that are grammatically in apposition or a direct object take a comma; quotations introduced by "that" (indirect discourse) take no quotation marks at all.
- **Syllable-division principles**: divide by word formation (know-ledge, not knowl-edge), divide on the vowel (edi-ble), divide between double letters unless they end the word's simple form (Apen-nines but tell-ing), and never divide before a silent final -ed (treat-ed, not roam-ed).

## Key Concepts
- **Heading**: leave a blank line (or equivalent space) after a manuscript's title before the body begins.
- **Numerals**: write dates and serial numbers in figures or Roman notation, never spelled out ("August 9, 1918," "Chapter XII").
- **Reference citation**: abbreviate frequently-recurring titles with a full list at the end; place citations in parentheses or footnotes, not the sentence body; omit "act," "scene," "line," etc. except when citing by only one of them.
- **Title formatting**: use italics with capitalized initials for literary titles as the default scholarly usage; drop an initial "A" or "The" from a title when placing the possessive before it ("Dickens's Tale of Two Cities").

## Mental Models
- Treat parenthetical material as a fully separate mini-sentence for internal punctuation purposes, then punctuate the carrier sentence as though the parenthesis were invisible.
- When formatting a reference or citation, ask "does the reader need this to locate the source, and nothing more?" — omit any label the numbers alone can carry.

## Anti-patterns
- **Spelling out dates/numbers that should be figures**: reduces precision and readability for no gain.
- **Inconsistent quotation-mark logic**: mixing colon-introduction, comma-introduction, and no-marks-for-indirect-discourse without regard for which grammatical relationship actually applies.
- **Cutting off a single letter or two letters of a long word when hyphenating** — both look awkward and should be avoided even when technically "room" exists at the line's end.

## Key Takeaways
1. Numerals, not spelled-out words, for dates and serial numbers.
2. Punctuate parenthetical asides as if they were two separate, self-contained sentences layered together — not as one continuous punctuation stream.
3. Match quotation-mark style to the grammatical role of the quotation: colon for formal/documentary, comma for apposition/direct object, none for indirect discourse with "that."
4. Default to italics with capitalized initials for titles of literary works.
5. Divide words at syllable boundaries that respect their formation and pronunciation, not just wherever the line happens to end.
