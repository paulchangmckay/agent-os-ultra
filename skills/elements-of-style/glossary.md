# Glossary — The Elements of Style

Alphabetical. Rule/word entries from Chapter V carry no chapter number (they're the chapter itself); structural terms cite the chapter they're introduced in.

**All right** — idiomatic only as a detached phrase meaning "agreed"; always two words, never "alright" (Ch 5)
**Comma splice** — joining two independent clauses with only a comma, forbidden by Rule 5 (Ch 2)
**Compare to / Compare with** — "to" for resemblance between different-order things; "with" for differences between same-order things (Ch 5)
**Data** — a plural noun, like phenomena and strata; "these data were," not "this data was" (Ch 5)
**Different than** — incorrect; use different from, other than, or unlike (Ch 5)
**Due to** — correct only as predicate/modifier of a specific noun, not as a loose substitute for "because of" (Ch 5)
**Fact** — reserve for matters of direct verification, not matters of judgment (Ch 5)
**Fewer / Less** — fewer for number, less for quantity (Ch 5)
**Filler noun** — case, character, factor, feature, nature, system: near-interchangeable padding words that rarely earn their place (Ch 5)
**Like / As** — "like" governs nouns/pronouns only; "as" introduces phrases and clauses (Ch 5)
**Logical predicate** — the "new," most important information in a sentence; belongs at the end for emphasis (Rule 18) (Ch 3)
**Loose sentence** — two co-ordinate clauses joined by and/but/so, grammatically complete before the sentence ends; fine occasionally, monotonous in a row (Rule 14) (Ch 2, Ch 3)
**Non-restrictive clause** — a clause that adds supplementary information about an already-identified antecedent; always set off by commas (Ch 2)
**Parenthetic expression** — a word, phrase, or clause that interrupts a sentence without being essential to its core meaning; enclosed by commas on both sides (Rule 3) (Ch 2)
**Periodic sentence** — a sentence that withholds its main clause/point until the end, creating emphasis through suspense (Ch 3)
**Recast-don't-patch rule** — when a word is misused, rewrite the whole sentence rather than swapping in a synonym (Ch 5)
**Restrictive clause** — a clause essential to identifying its antecedent; never set off by commas (Ch 2)
**Serial comma** — comma after every term in a series of three or more, except the last, when a single conjunction is used (Rule 2) (Ch 2)
**Shall / Will** — shall for first-person simple future belief, will for second/third person, or first-person determination (Ch 5)
**Should / Would** — should for first-person conditional or indirect quotation of "shall"; would for second/third person (Ch 5)
**Split infinitive** — an adverb interposed between "to" and its infinitive ("to diligently inquire"); in disfavor among careful writers (Ch 5)
**They (with singular antecedent)** — incorrect for distributive singular antecedents (each one, everybody); use "he" unless the antecedent must be feminine (Ch 5)
**Topic sentence** — the sentence, usually near the start of a paragraph, that states its main point (Rule 9) (Ch 3)
**Very** — an overused intensifier; reserve real emphasis for words strong in themselves (Ch 5)
**While** — reserve for its literal sense, "during the time that"; don't substitute for and/but/although (Ch 5)
**Whom** — don't substitute for "who" when "who" is the subject of a following verb, even before insertions like "he said" (Ch 5)
