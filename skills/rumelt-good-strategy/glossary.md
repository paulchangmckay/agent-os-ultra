# Glossary — Good Strategy / Bad Strategy

**Anomaly** — a fact that doesn't fit received wisdom; in business, the seed of a genuinely new strategic hypothesis (Ch 16).

**Anticipation** — insight into the predictable downstream results of events already in motion; a source of strategic leverage that doesn't require forecasting the unpredictable (Ch 6).

**Attractor state** — how an industry "should" evolve given technology and demand structure, based on overall efficiency rather than any one firm's ambition; a gravity-like pull, not a guarantee (Ch 13).

**Bad strategic objectives** — subgoals that are either a "dog's dinner" (unprioritized laundry list) or "blue sky" (as hard to achieve as the original challenge) (Ch 3).

**Blue-sky objective** — an objective that merely restates the desired end state with no theory of how to reach it (Ch 3, Ch 7).

**Chain-link logic** — a system whose performance is capped by its weakest link; strengthening other links adds nothing until the bottleneck is fixed (Ch 8).

**Coherent action** — a set of steps coordinated with one another to jointly execute a guiding policy; the third element of the kernel (Ch 5).

**Concentration** — focusing limited resources on fewer objectives to exceed a threshold effect rather than spreading effort thin (Ch 6).

**Corner solution** — a strategy that focuses on one aspect of a situation rather than compromising across many (Ch 17).

**Create-Destroy** — the discipline of actively trying to dismantle your own first strategic insight to force a better one to emerge (Ch 17).

**Design (strategy as)** — treating strategy as an adroit, mutually-adjusted configuration of resources and actions, closer to engineering than to choosing from a list of alternatives (Ch 9).

**Diagnosis** — the element of the kernel that defines the nature of a challenge by simplifying overwhelming complexity to its critical aspects (Ch 5).

**Dog's dinner objectives** — an unprioritized scramble of "things to do" from a planning meeting, mislabeled as strategy (Ch 3).

**Entropy** — the tendency of any unmanaged structure to become progressively less ordered over time, even absent external change (Ch 14).

**Fluff** — gibberish dressed as strategic insight using inflated "Sunday words" (Ch 3).

**Focus** — coordinating policies so their interacting effects produce extra power, aimed at a segment where that power creates more value than rivals can match (Ch 10).

**Gain to Operating (GTO)** — Rumelt's adjusted profit measure correcting for distorted capital-cost accounting across business units (Ch 14).

**Good strategy kernel** — the three-part core of any real strategy: diagnosis, guiding policy, coherent action (Ch 5).

**Guiding policy** — an overall approach for overcoming diagnosed obstacles; directs action like highway guardrails without specifying every move (Ch 5).

**Hump chart** — a chart ranking business units by profit contribution to reveal cross-subsidies masked in aggregate reporting (Ch 14).

**Inertia (of routine / culture / by proxy)** — three distinct forms of organizational resistance to change, each requiring a different fix (Ch 14).

**Inside view** — the tendency to believe "this case is different," discounting directly relevant historical base rates (Ch 18).

**Isolating mechanism** — a barrier (patent, reputation, network effect, tacit skill) preventing competitors from duplicating the resource behind an advantage (Ch 12).

**Kernel** — see Good strategy kernel.

**New Thought** — the doctrine that positive thinking alone produces success, a historical strain that seeps into "shared vision" strategy rhetoric (Ch 4).

**Pivot point** — a natural or created imbalance where a small push unleashes disproportionately large effect (Ch 6).

**Premeditation** — the requirement that a real strategy be planned in advance, not improvised (Ch 9).

**Proximate objective** — a target close enough at hand to be genuinely feasible with known methods (Ch 7).

**Quality matching** — the reason chain-linked systems stall: no incentive to improve one link while others lag (Ch 8).

**Sources of power** — the Part II framework set: leverage, proximate objectives, chain-link systems, design, focus, growth, advantage, dynamics, inertia, entropy (Ch 6–14).

**Strategic resource** — a long-lasting, hard-to-duplicate asset that confers advantage and that rivals can't match without net economic loss (Ch 9, Ch 12).

**Strategy-as-hypothesis** — treating a strategy as an educated, testable conjecture rather than a guaranteed formula (Ch 16).

**Template-style strategy** — filling in a vision/mission/values/strategies template as a substitute for real analysis (Ch 4).

**Wave of change** — a largely exogenous industry-wide shift (technology, regulation, cost structure) that a strategist rides rather than controls (Ch 13).

**Wrestling the gorilla** — competing in an arena where a rival's structural strength dominates yours, despite your own strength elsewhere (Ch 12).
