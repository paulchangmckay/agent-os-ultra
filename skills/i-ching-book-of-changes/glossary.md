# Glossary

**Central** — The second and fifth line positions; central within their own trigram regardless of firm/yielding character. Central position is favorable in most hexagrams even without "correctness" (Ch 67).

**Correct / Incorrect (line)** — A line is "correct" when a firm (yang) line sits in an odd position (1, 3, 5) or a yielding (yin) line sits in an even position (2, 4, 6). Correctness is necessary but not sufficient for a favorable reading — the hexagram's "time" can override it (Ch 67).

**Correspondence** — The relationship between lines in analogous places across the lower and upper trigram: 1st/4th, 2nd/5th, 3rd/top. Strongest and most informative between the 2nd and 5th lines (official/ruler); requires the two lines to differ in kind (one firm, one yielding) to count as true correspondence (Ch 67).

**Eight Houses** — An alternative organization of the 64 hexagrams into 8 families of 8, each family grouped under one of the pure (doubled) trigrams as its "house" (Appendix II).

**Hexagram (kua)** — A six-line figure built from two trigrams (upper and lower), the basic unit of the I Ching's 64-part system.

**Holding Together** — The relationship between two *adjacent* lines of differing character. The 4th/5th pair (minister/ruler) is the most consequential case: a yielding minister holding together with a strong ruler is usually favorable (Ch 67).

**Judgment (T'uan)** — The core pronouncement on a hexagram as a whole, traditionally attributed to King Wên. Answers "what does this whole situation mean?" (Book I).

**King Wên sequence** — The traditional ordering of the 64 hexagrams, numbered 1–64, used throughout this translation and by the `cast.py` tool (Ch 65; Appendix II).

**Moving line (changing line)** — A line cast as old yin (6) or old yang (9); it is at its extreme and transforms into its opposite, producing a resulting hexagram. Static lines (young yin 8, young yang 7) do not change (Ta Chuan; Appendix I).

**Nuclear trigrams (hu kua)** — The two trigrams hidden inside a hexagram's four middle lines (positions 2–5): the lower nuclear trigram from lines 2-3-4, the upper from lines 3-4-5. A second, deeper layer of meaning beneath the two primary trigrams (Ch 67).

**Old yang / Old yin** — Coin-sum values of 9 and 6 respectively; both are moving lines (Appendix I).

**Places (the six line positions)** — Each of a hexagram's six positions carries a fixed significance: 1st = beginning, not yet in the situation; 2nd = official, central, usually favorable; 3rd = transitional, the riskiest position; 4th = minister, close to the ruler, requires caution; 5th = the ruler's place, central and strong by default; 6th (top) = withdrawn from the affairs of the time (Ch 67).

**Ruling line (constituting ruler vs. governing ruler)** — Two distinct concepts. The *constituting* ruler is whichever line gives the hexagram its defining meaning (not necessarily an admirable line). The *governing* ruler is the line — usually the 5th place — that is genuinely good and rules by both position and the demands of the time. The two can be the same line or different lines (Ch 67).

**Sequence of Earlier Heaven (Primal Arrangement)** — The trigram layout attributed to Fu Hsi, arranged as opposite pairs around a static, cosmological axis; read for underlying, timeless polarity (Ch 65).

**Sequence of Later Heaven (Inner-World Arrangement)** — King Wên's trigram layout, sequenced through the year's seasonal cycle; read for a hexagram's position in unfolding time (Ch 65).

**Shuo Kua** — "Discussion of the Trigrams," one of the Ten Wings; covers trigram attributes, images, family roles, and symbols (Ch 65).

**Superior man (chün-tzu)** — The ethical exemplar each hexagram's Image repeatedly counsels; originally meant "aristocrat" in early Chou China, broadened over time to mean a person of cultivated character.

**Synchronicity** — C. G. Jung's term (from his Foreword) for the I Ching's operating principle: meaningful coincidence between the moment of casting and the situation it describes, as distinct from causality.

**Ta Chuan (Hsi Tz'u Chuan)** — "The Great Treatise" or "Commentary on the Appended Judgments," one of the Ten Wings; the philosophical/cosmological core of the system — Tao, yin/yang, the sage's use of the oracle (Ch 66).

**Tao** — "That which lets now the dark, now the light appear" — the regulated alternation of yin and yang itself, not either force dominating (Ch 66).

**Ten Wings** — The ten commentary texts (T'uan Chuan, Hsiang Chuan, Wên Yen, Shuo Kua, Hsü Kua, Tsa Kua, Ta Chuan, and others) appended to the older core text, traditionally associated with Confucius though modern scholarship dates most of them later.

**The Image (Ta Hsiang, "Great Image")** — Richard Wilhelm's term for each hexagram's counsel to the "superior man," derived from the natural scene the two trigrams depict together (Book I; Hellmut Wilhelm's Preface).

**The Lines** — The six individual line-texts of a hexagram, traditionally attributed to the Duke of Chou, each describing that line's specific situation (Book I).

**Time** — The situation-type a whole hexagram represents: a movement (growth/decrease), a characteristic action or process, a law or principle, or a symbolic situation as such. Determines how a given line value should be read (Ch 67).

**Trigram (kua)** — A three-line figure of firm (unbroken, yang) and yielding (broken, yin) lines; two trigrams combine to form one hexagram (Ch 65).

**T'uan Chuan** — "Commentary on the Decision," one of the Ten Wings; supplies the deeper structural reasoning behind each hexagram's Judgment, found in Book III under each hexagram (Ch 1 and throughout Book III).

**Wên Yen** — "Commentary on the Words of the Text," one of the Ten Wings, applied only to hexagrams 1 (Ch'ien) and 2 (K'un) (Ch 1, Ch 2).

**Yarrow-stalk oracle / Coin oracle** — The two traditional casting methods. The yarrow-stalk method uses 49 stalks divided and counted through three rounds per line; the coin method (used by `cast.py`) throws three coins per line, six lines total (Appendix I).

**Yin / Yang** — The two primal, alternating forces: yin is yielding, dark, receptive; yang is firm, light, active. Every line, trigram, and hexagram is built from their combination (Ch 65, Ch 66).

**Young yang / Young yin** — Coin-sum values of 7 and 8 respectively; both are static (non-moving) lines (Appendix I).
