# Hexagram 23: Po / Splitting Apart

## Core Idea
Splitting Apart pictures inferior, dark forces overcoming the strong not by direct confrontation but by gradual, imperceptible undermining — like a house whose roof is collapsing — so the wise response is to submit to the bad time and remain quiet rather than resist directly.

## Trigrams
- Upper: Kên, the mountain
- Lower: K'un, the earth

## The Judgment
> SPLITTING APART. It does not further one to go anywhere.

**Meaning**: When inferior forces are ascendant and crowding out the strong, undertaking anything is unfavorable; this is a matter of time conditions, not personal failing, so submission is wisdom, not cowardice.

## The Image
> The mountain rests on the earth: the image of SPLITTING APART. Thus those above can ensure their position only by giving generously to those below.

**Meaning**: A mountain that is steep and narrow topples, but one that rises broad and great from the earth stands secure; likewise rulers secure their position only by being as generous and benevolent as the earth that carries all.

## The Lines
- **Six at the beginning**: "The leg of the bed is split. Those who persevere are destroyed. Misfortune." — Inferior forces begin stealthy, destructive undermining from below; the situation bodes disaster, and there is nothing to do but wait.
- **Six in the second place**: "The bed is split at the edge. Those who persevere are destroyed. Misfortune." — Danger draws closer and one stands isolated without help from above or below; stubborn perseverance in one's position would lead to downfall, so caution and adjustment to the time are needed.
- **Six in the third place**: "He splits with them. No blame." — Committed by circumstance to an evil environment but bound by an inner relationship to a superior man, one gains the stability to break from those around, and this opposition is not wrong.
- **Six in the fourth place**: "The bed is split up to the skin. Misfortune." — Disaster now reaches the person, not just the resting place; misfortune has peaked and can no longer be warded off.
- **Six in the fifth place**: "A shoal of fishes. Favor comes through the court ladies. Everything acts to further." — Close to the strong principle at the top, the dark force changes character, voluntarily submitting to and leading the other weak lines toward the strong one, and this cooperative shift brings good to all.
- **Nine at the top**: "There is a large fruit still uneaten. The superior man receives a carriage. The house of the inferior man is split apart." — When splitting apart has spent itself, the seed of good remains and sprouts anew; the superior man regains influence, supported by public opinion, while the inferior man's wickedness returns upon himself.

## Confucian Commentary Highlights
- The nine at the top is the hexagram's ruler: even though the dark force splinters the light throughout the hexagram, the light principle cannot be wholly split apart, and this single surviving strong line anchors the entire structure.
- The Miscellaneous Notes tie this hexagram directly to the next (hexagram 24, Return): "splitting apart means decay," and decay must occur before new seed can develop — the fruit falling to the ground is what allows new life to sprout.
- Structurally, both nuclear trigrams and the lower primary trigram are all K'un (downward-moving), while the upper trigram Kên stands motionless — creating a loosening, sinking tendency that threatens to take the ground from under the single remaining strong line at the top.
- The Commentary reframes the Image's focus: it is not really about the waxing and waning of dark and light forces, but about the solidity of the foundation — generous giving (the earth's nature) is what produces the assured calm of the mountain's nature, preventing the splitting apart in the first place.

## Worked Example
Take the six in the fifth place: "A shoal of fishes. Favor comes through the court ladies. Everything acts to further." The commentary explains that at this point the yin line, now closest to the single remaining strong line, changes its posture entirely — instead of continuing the destructive undermining of the lines below it, it subordinates itself and leads the other yin lines toward the strong line, like a queen leading her court to her husband. This deepens the Judgment's overall counsel of quiet submission: even within a time of decline, cooperation with what remains strong, rather than continued opposition, is what allows a good outcome to emerge before the cycle turns.

## Key Takeaways
- When the general trend is decline, don't fight it directly — submit, remain quiet, and wait rather than force outcomes.
- Secure your position through generosity and a broad foundation, not through rigid narrowness or self-protection alone.
- Recognize isolation as a warning sign; without support from above or below, stubborn persistence increases danger.
- Loyalty to what's genuinely good may put you at odds with your immediate environment — that opposition is not a fault.
- Trust that decay carries the seed of renewal; even at the worst point, what is genuinely good survives to sprout again.
