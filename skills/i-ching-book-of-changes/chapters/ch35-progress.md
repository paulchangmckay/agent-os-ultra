# Hexagram 35: Chin / Progress

## Core Idea
The sun rising over the earth pictures rapid, easy advancement built on the right relationship between a wise, generous ruler above and a loyal, clear-sighted servant below.

## Trigrams
- Upper: Li (the Clinging, Light, Fire)
- Lower: K'un (the Receptive, Earth)

## The Judgment
> PROGRESS. The powerful prince is honored with horses in large numbers. In a single day he is granted audience three times.

**Meaning**: A capable person in a subordinate position gains wide trust and uses that influence for the benefit of the one above him, who in turn rewards him generously and draws him close — mutual good faith between leader and follower is what makes progress possible.

## The Image
> The sun rises over the earth: the image of PROGRESS. Thus the superior man himself brightens his bright virtue.

**Meaning**: Just as the sun's light grows clearer and wider as it climbs free of the morning mists, a person's originally good nature grows clearer through deliberate self-purification.

## The Lines
- **Six at the beginning**: "Progressing, but turned back. Perseverance brings good fortune. If one meets with no confidence, one should remain calm. No mistake." — Facing a rebuff to advancement, simply persevering in what is right, staying calm rather than forcing trust, avoids error.
- **Six in the second place**: "Progressing, but in sorrow. Perseverance brings good fortune. Then one obtains great happiness from one's ancestress." — When progress is halted and one is grieved, persevering brings, through principled connection rather than partisan motive, great eventual happiness.
- **Six in the third place**: "All are in accord. Remorse disappears." — Striving in association with like-minded others dispels the regret of lacking the independent strength to triumph alone.
- **Nine in the fourth place**: "Progress like a hamster. Perseverance brings danger." — Amassing gains in the wrong position, shunning the light like a hamster, brings danger precisely because a time of progress inevitably exposes dubious conduct.
- **Six in the fifth place**: "Remorse disappears. Take not gain and loss to heart. Undertakings bring good fortune. Everything serves to further." — In an influential position, remaining gentle and not fixating on personal gain or loss secures the greater good of continued beneficent influence.
- **Nine at the top**: "Making progress with the horns is permissible only for the purpose of punishing one's own city. To be conscious of danger brings good fortune. No blame. Perseverance brings humiliation." — Offensive force is permissible only to correct one's own people, and even then only with full awareness of its danger; used elsewhere, it leads to humiliation.

## Confucian Commentary Highlights
- The ruler of the hexagram is the yielding six in the fifth place, which "progresses and goes upward" precisely because it occupies the middle of the upper trigram Li — the wise-ruler image in the Judgment is built directly on this line's structural position.
- The text explicitly ranks three progress-themed hexagrams by the gradualness of their advance: Chin (this one, sun over earth) as the finest and fastest, Shêng (46, wood over earth) as slower, and Chien (53, a tree on a mountain) as slowest — noting too-rapid expansion carries its own dangers, foreshadowing Hexagram 36.
- The fourth line's "hamster" image is explained structurally: this line sits atop the nuclear trigram Kên (associated with rodents) yet already touches the trigram of the sun, whose light it cannot bear — a strong line badly placed, doubly dangerous because it also forms part of the nuclear trigram K'an (the Abysmal, danger).
- The fifth line's freedom from regret over "gain and loss" is tied to fire's nature: flame has no fixed form, flaring and dying, so the ruler who embodies this trigram is naturally undisturbed by transient gain and loss.

## Worked Example
Consider the nine in the fourth place: "Progress like a hamster. Perseverance brings danger." Someone advancing quickly in a favorable period may be tempted to quietly accumulate advantage — position, credit, resources — while avoiding scrutiny, the way a hamster hoards and hides by day. This line warns that periods of visible, general progress are exactly the periods in which hidden, self-interested conduct gets exposed; persevering in it only compounds the danger. It deepens the Judgment's picture of progress as a relationship built on trust: this line shows what betrays that trust and why the timing makes betrayal especially costly.

## Key Takeaways
- When advancement stalls, respond with calm perseverance rather than forcing confidence you have not yet earned.
- Seek the backing of like-minded others when your own strength is not enough to carry you through resistance alone.
- Do not hoard advantage quietly during a favorable period — visibility works both ways, and concealment invites exposure.
- In an influential position, hold gain and loss lightly; steady, ungrasping conduct preserves your capacity for good influence longer than short-term wins would.
- Reserve any forceful, offensive action for correcting your own side, and only ever with full awareness of its risk.

## Connects To
- Explicitly ranked alongside Hexagram 46 (Shêng, Pushing Upward) and Hexagram 53 (Chien, Development) as related "progress" hexagrams of differing gradualness.
- Directly precedes and contrasts with Hexagram 36 (Ming I, Darkening of the Light), its structural inverse.
