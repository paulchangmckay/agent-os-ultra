# Hexagram 31: Hsien / Influence (Wooing)

## Core Idea
Influence is the universal, unconscious, involuntary stimulation between complementary forces — pictured as courtship, where the strong masculine principle takes initiative by placing itself below the weak feminine principle, and cheerful mutual response follows.

## Trigrams
- Upper: Tui, the Joyous (lake)
- Lower: Kên, Keeping Still (mountain)

## The Judgment
> Influence. Success. Perseverance furthers. To take a maiden to wife brings good fortune.

**Meaning**: The weak above and strong below attract each other into union; keeping still within while joyous without keeps that joy from excess, and this same law of mutual attraction is how heaven and earth bring forth all creatures and how the sage brings peace to the world.

## The Image
> A lake on the mountain: the image of influence. Thus the superior man encourages people to approach him by his readiness to receive them.

**Meaning**: A mountain can hold a lake on its summit only because that summit is sunken, not jutting; likewise the mind must stay humble and empty in order to remain receptive to good advice, since no one keeps counseling a man who thinks he already knows everything.

## The Lines
- **Six at the beginning**: "The influence shows itself in the big toe." — An intention exists but has no visible effect yet, so it is of no consequence to others, for good or ill.
- **Six in the second place**: "The influence shows itself in the calves of the legs. Misfortune. Tarrying brings good fortune." — Movement that isn't self-governed bodes ill; waiting quietly until impelled by a genuine influence keeps one uninjured.
- **Nine in the third place**: "The influence shows itself in the thighs. Holds to that which follows it. To continue is humiliating." — Acting on the spur of every caprice, running after whoever one wants to influence or yielding to every whim, leads to humiliation if continued; holding back is the mark of freedom.
- **Nine in the fourth place**: "Perseverance brings good fortune. Remorse disappears. If a man is agitated in mind, and his thoughts go hither and thither, only those friends on whom he fixes his conscious thoughts will follow." — Constant, good influence from the heart avoids remorse, but influence should never become a conscious, willed effort to manipulate others — that only exhausts the influencer and limits the effect to those consciously targeted.
- **Nine in the fifth place**: "The influence shows itself in the back of the neck. No remorse." — Influence rooted in the most rigid, steady part of the body keeps the will firm and free from confusion, arising from what cannot be consciously called forth or prevented.
- **Six at the top**: "The influence shows itself in the jaws, cheeks, and tongue." — Mere talk without substance behind it produces only insignificant influence, which is why no fortune, good or bad, is even attached to this line.

## Confucian Commentary Highlights
- The nine in the fourth place, at the place of the heart, is a ruler of the hexagram because "the heart holds mastery in influence"; the nine in the fifth place, at the place of the back, is ruler to a still greater degree, since it represents keeping still in the very midst of movement.
- The Commentary distinguishes the hexagram's name (hsien, meaning stimulation) from the character kan ("to stimulate") by noting hsien has no heart component — marking the influence described here as unconscious and involuntary, an objective relationship of a general kind rather than a subjective, willed one.
- The Sequence traces a chain of necessity from heaven and earth, to individual things, to the two sexes, to husband and wife, to father and son, to prince and servitor, to superior and inferior, to the rules of propriety and right — positioning courtship as the very foundation of all social relationship, opening the second part of the book just as heaven and earth open the first.
- On the fourth line, Confucius is quoted reinforcing that true influence needs no conscious contrivance: "What need has nature of thought and care?... through one action, the fruits of a hundred thoughts are realized."

## Worked Example
Take the nine in the fourth place: "Perseverance brings good fortune. Remorse disappears... Only those friends on whom he fixes his conscious thoughts will follow." The commentary explains this line has a twofold possibility: it can remain firm, quietly influencing others simply through its own character (in harmony with the right, stirring up nothing injurious), or it can slip into influence by conscious, willed effort — which limits its effect only to those deliberately targeted, and darkens its own inner light in the process. This deepens the Judgment's whole picture of influence: the more genuine kind works through quiet consistency of character, not manipulation, and manipulation actually narrows and weakens the very effect it's trying to produce.

## Key Takeaways
- Real influence over others starts from unforced, unconscious consistency of character, not calculated persuasion.
- Stay receptive and humble like the hollowed mountain summit; a mind that thinks it knows everything stops attracting good counsel.
- Don't act on every impulse to sway or follow others — the capacity to hold back is the foundation of freedom.
- Beware of consciously trying to manipulate people's hearts; it exhausts you and limits your effect to only those you're directly targeting.
- Recognize that mere talk, however persuasive-sounding, produces only shallow, insignificant influence without real substance behind it.
