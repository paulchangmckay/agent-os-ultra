# Hexagram 56: Lü / The Wanderer

## Core Idea
The condition of being a stranger without a fixed home — success here comes only through modesty, caution, and restraint, never through boldness or self-assertion.

## Trigrams
- Upper: Li (Fire)
- Lower: Kên (Mountain, Keeping Still)

## The Judgment
> THE WANDERER. Success through smallness. Perseverance brings good fortune To the wanderer.

**Meaning**: A person without a settled place or large circle of support must stay cautious, reserved, and unassuming — obliging toward others rather than overbearing — since only that stance protects him and brings success.

## The Image
> Fire on the mountain: The image of THE WANDERER. Thus the superior man Is clear-minded and cautious In imposing penalties, And protracts no lawsuits.

**Meaning**: Just as fire on a mountain flares briefly and moves on rather than lingering, legal matters and penalties should be resolved quickly and not dragged out — prisons are meant to be temporary, not permanent dwellings.

## The Lines
- **Six at the beginning**: "If the wanderer busies himself with trivial things, He draws down misfortune upon himself." — A stranger who demeans himself with trivial jokes or buffoonery to win favor only invites contempt; preserve inner dignity instead.
- **Six in the second place**: "The wanderer comes to an inn. He has his property with him. He wins the steadfastness of a young servant." — Modesty and reserve let a wanderer find a resting place, keep his resources, and even win a loyal, trustworthy helper.
- **Nine in the third place**: "The wanderer's inn burns down. He loses the steadfastness of his young servant. Danger." — Arrogance and meddling in matters that don't concern you costs a wanderer both his shelter and the loyalty of those beneath him — real danger follows.
- **Nine in the fourth place**: "The wanderer rests in a shelter. He obtains his property and an ax. My heart is not glad." — Even limiting your desires and securing a temporary shelter and means of defense, a wanderer's situation stays inherently unsettled — full ease is not yet available.
- **Six in the fifth place**: "He shoots a pheasant. It drops with the first arrow. In the end this brings both praise and office." — Knowing the right way to introduce and prove yourself in a foreign situation can win real recognition and standing, even far from home.
- **Nine at the top**: "The bird's nest burns up. The wanderer laughs at first, Then must needs lament and weep. Through carelessness he loses his cow. Misfortune." — Forgetting your position, indulging in careless gaiety, and losing your adaptability (the "cow") brings loss of your very foothold and eventual grief.

## Confucian Commentary Highlights
- The T'uan Chuan reads the ruling six in the fifth place as embodying the whole hexagram's lesson: yielding, central, submitting to the strong lines around it — precisely this humility is what keeps a wanderer from being humiliated in a foreign land.
- Book III states directly, "the meaning of the time of THE WANDERER is truly great" — grasping the meaning of one's displaced, unsettled moment is itself the real challenge and achievement.
- The hexagram is explicitly the structural inverse of Fêng, Abundance (55) — where Abundance's two trigrams reinforce each other into a peak, here Li (fire) and Kên (mountain) actively pull apart, mirroring the wanderer's lack of a stable home.
- Throughout, fire (Li) is read as primarily a "consuming, injurious force" rather than illuminating — explaining why the bottom line's link to it is harmful rather than clarifying, and why the top line's fire burns up its own nest.

## Worked Example
Take the nine in the third place: "The wanderer's inn burns down. He loses the steadfastness of his young servant. Danger." Book III explains this line is "hard in a strong place" — too rigid, showing no devotion to those above or below it. Because it treats its subordinate "like a stranger," it rightly loses that loyalty, and its shelter (the inn) burns down as a direct consequence. Applied practically: when you are in an unfamiliar or precarious position and treat the people who could support you with arrogance or aloofness, you actively destroy your own safety net — the danger here is self-inflicted, not circumstantial.

## Key Takeaways
- In an unfamiliar or unstable situation, lead with modesty and reserve rather than assertiveness — it is what actually protects you.
- Do not try to win favor by demeaning yourself with trivial jokes or flattery; it invites contempt, not goodwill.
- Treat those who serve or support you with respect, even temporarily — losing their loyalty removes your safety net exactly when you need it.
- Even once you find shelter and resources, stay alert; a wanderer's situation is inherently provisional, not yet fully secure.
- Guard against careless indulgence once things go well — forgetting your actual position is what turns early laughter into later grief.

## Connects To
- Fêng (55), Abundance — the hexagram immediately preceding this one in the Sequence, and its structural inverse: "whatever greatness may exhaust itself upon, this much is certain: it loses its home," directly explaining why Abundance gives way to the Wanderer.
