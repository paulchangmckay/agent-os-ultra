# Hexagram 57: Sun / The Gentle (Penetrating, Wind)

## Core Idea
Gentle, persistent, non-forceful influence — like wind or growing wood — that succeeds through gradual, repeated penetration rather than sudden confrontation, and works best when subordinated to a clear goal and a capable leader.

## Trigrams
- Upper: Sun (Wind, the Gentle)
- Lower: Sun (Wind, the Gentle)

## The Judgment
> THE GENTLE. Success through what is small. It furthers one to have somewhere to go. It furthers one to see the great man.

**Meaning**: Gentle, sustained influence produces results that are less dramatic but more enduring than force — success requires a clearly defined goal and, for those of small strength, subordinating oneself to a capable leader.

## The Image
> Winds following one upon the other: The image of THE GENTLY PENETRATING. Thus the superior man Spreads his commands abroad And carries out his undertakings.

**Meaning**: Like wind that persists ceaselessly, a leader's influence must be sustained and repeated until it is genuinely absorbed by people — only then can real action follow; action without that groundwork just frightens and repels.

## The Lines
- **Six at the beginning**: "In advancing and in retreating, The perseverance of a warrior furthers." — Gentle natures prone to indecision need imposed discipline; resolute steadiness beats irresolute drifting.
- **Nine in the second place**: "Penetration under the bed. Priests and magicians are used in great number. Good fortune. No blame." — Hidden, intangible influences require vigorous, thorough effort to trace to their source and expose; bringing them into the light strips them of their power.
- **Nine in the third place**: "Repeated penetration. Humiliation." — Endless deliberation without a resulting decision breeds fresh doubt and humiliation; once a matter is thoroughly considered, act.
- **Six in the fourth place**: "Remorse vanishes. During the hunt Three kinds of game are caught." — Combining innate modesty with energetic action, from a position of responsibility and experience, produces comprehensive success.
- **Nine in the fifth place**: "Perseverance brings good fortune. Remorse vanishes. Nothing that does not further. No beginning, but an end. Before the change, three days. After the change, three days. Good fortune." — Reform (not a fresh start) requires steadfastness and careful thought both before and after the change is made; test the results carefully once enacted.
- **Nine at the top**: "Penetration under the bed. He loses his property and his ax. Perseverance brings misfortune." — Understanding can penetrate deep into hidden matters, but without the strength left to act decisively on what's found, persisting only brings harm.

## Confucian Commentary Highlights
- The T'uan Chuan identifies the nine in the fifth place as the true governing ruler — only from an honored, central position can commands actually "penetrate to the middle and the correct" and be carried out.
- Book III explains the doubled trigram structure as a "twofold penetration": first reaching the felt understanding of subordinates (dispersing hidden resistance like wind scatters clouds), then reaching a deeper level of consciousness where genuine good is awakened — hence the need for repeated, not single, commands.
- The Miscellaneous Notes gloss Sun as "crouching" — the dark (yielding) line beneath the strong lines succeeds precisely by gentle submission, not by contest.
- The fifth line's cryptic "before the change, three days; after the change, three days" is explained via the cyclic sign Kêng: reform requires decisively abandoning a flawed beginning to reach a good end, differing from Ku (18) where the situation sits exactly between an ending and a beginning.

## Worked Example
Take the nine in the third place: "Repeated penetration. Humiliation." Book III explains this line sits at the junction of the two Sun trigrams — one ending, one just starting — and is "too hard and not central," meaning its character doesn't actually suit gentle, patient penetration even though it keeps attempting it. The result is that "the will exhausts itself" in endless vacillation. Applied practically: this line warns against mistaking repeated reconsideration for genuine gentleness — true Sun-style influence works through sustained, consistent direction, not through restarting the same deliberation over and over. If you notice yourself re-litigating a decision without ever committing, that's this line's warning, not virtue.

## Key Takeaways
- Sustained, patient influence outlasts and outperforms forceful confrontation — but it needs a clear, unwavering direction to work.
- When facing hidden resistance or hidden agendas, thorough investigation that exposes them to the light is what neutralizes their power.
- Deliberate fully, then act — repeated second-guessing after a decision has been reasoned through produces only humiliation, not better outcomes.
- Reform (correcting an existing situation) requires care on both sides of the change: think it through beforehand, then verify its results afterward.
- Recognize the limit of insight without follow-through — seeing a problem clearly is not enough if you no longer have the resolve to act on it.

## Connects To
- Lü (56), The Wanderer — the hexagram immediately preceding this one in the Sequence: "the wanderer has nothing that might receive him; hence there follows the hexagram of THE GENTLE" — Sun represents the wanderer finally finding a way in, a kind of homecoming.
- Ku (18), Work on What Has Been Spoiled — contrasted directly in Book III: Ku addresses a situation squarely between an ending and new beginning, while the fifth line of Sun addresses reform where "there is no beginning, but an end."
