# Hexagram 11: T'ai / Peace

## Core Idea
Heaven placed beneath the earth: the Creative rises, the Receptive sinks, and their movements meet in deep harmony, producing a season — in nature, in society, in a life — where the good elements are ascendant and the inferior elements are in willing retreat.

## Trigrams
- Upper: K'un (Earth, devotion)
- Lower: Ch'ien (Heaven, strength)

## The Judgment
> PEACE. The small departs, the great approaches. Good fortune. Success.

**Meaning**: This marks a season of social harmony in which those in high places favor the lowly and the lowly are well-disposed toward the highly placed — the good elements of society are in a central, influential position and the inferior elements come under their beneficial influence.

## The Image
> Heaven and earth unite: the image of PEACE. Thus the ruler divides and completes the course of heaven and earth; he furthers and regulates the gifts of heaven and earth, and so aids the people.

**Meaning**: When nature is at its most abundant, human effort must both bound that abundance (through seasons, calendars, order) and actively further it (by matching effort to the right time and place), so its benefits actually reach people.

## The Lines
- **Nine at the beginning**: "When ribbon grass is pulled up, the sod comes with it. Each according to his kind. Undertakings bring good fortune." — In a time of prosperity, a capable person naturally draws like-minded people along when called to action, and setting out to accomplish something succeeds.
- **Nine in the second place**: "Bearing with the uncultured in gentleness, fording the river with resolution, not neglecting what is distant, not regarding one's companions: thus one may manage to walk in the middle." — Maintaining generosity toward imperfect people, courage for necessary risks, attention to what's far away, and independence from cliquish loyalty together keep one on the balanced middle path even in good times.
- **Nine in the third place**: "No plain not followed by a slope. No going not followed by a return. He who remains persevering in danger is without blame. Do not complain about this truth; enjoy the good fortune you still possess." — Since prosperity is inevitably followed by decline, staying mindful of that truth without falling into illusion, and simply enjoying present good fortune, is what keeps one blameless.
- **Six in the fourth place**: "He flutters down, not boasting of his wealth, together with his neighbor, guileless and sincere." — In a time of mutual trust, those of high rank approach the lowly simply and without ostentation, from genuine inner conviction rather than external pressure.
- **Six in the fifth place**: "The sovereign I gives his daughter in marriage. This brings blessing and supreme good fortune." — A modest, willing union between high and low rank — exemplified by a ruler's daughter marrying beneath her station — brings blessing and the fulfillment of what is truly desired.
- **Six at the top**: "The wall falls back into the moat. Use no army now. Make your commands known within your own town. Perseverance brings humiliation." — When the turning point toward decline has arrived, submit to it rather than resisting by force; hold only your own intimate circle together, since forceful resistance would only deepen the collapse.

## Confucian Commentary Highlights
- The nine in the second place and the six in the fifth place are both the constituting and governing rulers of the hexagram: the official fulfills his duty toward the ruler, and the ruler fulfills his duty toward subordinates, together expressing the union of "above" and "below" in one will.
- The T'uan Chuan reads the hexagram as a calendar image of ascending good and waning ill: the strong lines mount from below while the weak lines withdraw above, so "the small departs, the great approaches" describes an actual movement, not just a static balance — this hexagram corresponds to the first month, the start of spring.
- The commentary draws out a layered political meaning from the trigram positions: light (yang) within and dark (yin) without corresponds to strength within and devotion without, and further to superior men at the center of influence with inferior men on the periphery, subject to their good influence — "none of this occurs arbitrarily; it is born of the time."
- The Miscellaneous Notes state directly that Peace (T'ai) and Standstill (Hexagram 12, P'i) stand in natural opposition to one another — the two hexagrams are explicitly paired as structural and thematic opposites.
- Line-level commentary on the third line locates it precisely "on the boundary between heaven and earth, between yang and yin" — the exact turning point of the hexagram's arc — explaining why it counsels neither despair nor complacency but persevering awareness.

## Worked Example
Take the nine in the third place: "No plain not followed by a slope. No going not followed by a return." The commentary places this line exactly at the structural midpoint of the hexagram, the boundary where the ascending and descending movements meet — the precise place where the turn from prosperity toward decline begins to be felt. Rather than reading this as bad news, the line instructs the reader to remain strong and simply enjoy the good fortune still present, without complaint about the underlying truth that all conditions eventually reverse. It shows that awareness of coming change, held without either despair or denial, is itself what keeps a person blameless through the transition.

## Key Takeaways
- Recognize a season of genuine flourishing when leaders and subordinates, or any two unequal parties, are working toward the same goal in mutual goodwill.
- In good times, actively draw capable, like-minded people into shared undertakings — a rising tide should be used to accomplish something, not merely enjoyed alone.
- Guard prosperity with four disciplines: tolerance for others' imperfections, willingness to take necessary risks, attention to what's easy to neglect, and freedom from clique loyalty.
- Hold the plain truth that every plain is followed by a slope without letting it curdle into either denial or gloom — enjoy present good fortune while staying realistic about its limits.
- When you have real standing, approach those with less openly and without flaunting your position — genuine, unpretentious contact is what makes unequal relationships work.
- Once decline has genuinely begun, don't try to force a reversal through aggressive resistance — consolidate what's closest to you and let the larger movement complete itself.
