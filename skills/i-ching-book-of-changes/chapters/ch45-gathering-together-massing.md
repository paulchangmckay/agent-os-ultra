# Hexagram 45: Ts'ui / Gathering Together [Massing]

## Core Idea
People naturally mass together around a strong leader and shared reverence — but wherever large numbers gather, strife and danger also gather, so the wise response is both to unify around a true center and to prepare for the unforeseen.

## Trigrams
- Upper: Tui (the Lake, the Joyous)
- Lower: K'un (Earth, the Receptive)

## The Judgment
> GATHERING TOGETHER. Success. The king approaches his temple. It furthers one to see the great man. This brings success. Perseverance furthers. To bring great offerings creates good fortune. It furthers one to undertake something.

**Meaning**: Gathering people together, whether in a family or a state, requires both religious/spiritual force and a human leader who is himself collected within — only such collective moral force, expressed through great offerings befitting the moment, can truly unite people for great undertakings.

## The Image
> Over the earth, the lake: the image of GATHERING TOGETHER. Thus the superior man renews his weapons in order to meet the unforeseen.

**Meaning**: Just as a lake risen above the earth threatens to break through, wherever people and possessions gather in great numbers, strife and robbery become likely — so preparedness against the unexpected must accompany any gathering.

## The Lines
- **Six at the beginning**: "If you are sincere, but not to the end, there will sometimes be confusion, sometimes gathering together. If you call out, then after one grasp of the hand you can laugh again. Regret not. Going is without blame." — Wavering under the influence of a larger group, one need only call out for help; a single grasp of the leader's hand is enough to dispel confusion.
- **Six in the second place**: "Letting oneself be drawn brings good fortune and remains blameless. If one is sincere, it furthers one to bring even a small offering." — In a time of gathering together, yielding to a natural, secret attraction toward those one belongs with avoids mistakes; sincerity makes even a small offering acceptable.
- **Six in the third place**: "Gathering together amid sighs. Nothing that would further. Going is without blame. Slight humiliation." — Isolated because others have already formed their own group, one should resolutely seek a connection to someone nearer the center; this is not a mistake even if initially somewhat humiliating.
- **Nine in the fourth place**: "Great good fortune. No blame." — Gathering people together on behalf of one's ruler, without seeking personal advantage, brings great good fortune because the work is unselfish.
- **Nine in the fifth place**: "If in gathering together one has position, this brings no blame. If there are some who are not yet sincerely in the work, sublime and enduring perseverance is needed. Then remorse disappears." — Position alone may draw people, some without real confidence; only steadfast, unswerving devotion to duty overcomes their secret mistrust.
- **Six at the top**: "Lamenting and sighing, floods of tears. No blame." — Good intentions toward an alliance, when misunderstood, may cause real sadness, but this very lament can bring the other person to their senses, achieving the alliance after all.

## Confucian Commentary Highlights
- The Commentary on the Decision derives the hexagram's meaning from the two trigram attributes: "Devoted and at the same time joyous. The strong stands in the middle and finds correspondence. Therefore the others mass around it" — the gathering is explained as a direct structural consequence of the ruling nine in the fifth place's position.
- The hexagram is explicitly related to Hexagram 8 (Pi, Holding Together), sharing its basic pattern of massing around a strong center, but distinguished because a lake collecting water above the earth expresses "gathering together" even more strongly than water simply lying over the earth.
- The text notes a religious dimension underlying all gathering: "Heaven is the bond of union in nature, as the ancestors are the bond of union among men" — the great offerings mentioned in the Judgment are tied directly to this cross-generational, spiritual basis for community.
- The Image's warning about renewed weapons is explained through trigram attributes rather than left as a generic warning: Tui (metal, weapons) and K'un (renewal, since earth produces metal) combine to show that preparedness against unforeseen danger is built into the very structure of the hexagram.

## Worked Example
Consider the nine in the fifth place: "If in gathering together one has position, this brings no blame. If there are some who are not yet sincerely in the work, sublime and enduring perseverance is needed. Then remorse disappears." A leader who has drawn followers purely through authority or title — a new manager, an inherited position of trust — may find that some of those gathered around aren't there out of real confidence but simply because of the position itself. This line shows the remedy is not to demand loyalty but to earn it, through sustained, unwavering devotion to the work over time. It adds nuance to the Judgment's emphasis on the "great man" as the necessary center of gathering: holding a central position is only the starting condition: real, lasting unity still has to be built through demonstrated character.

## Key Takeaways
- When you feel your commitment wavering under the pull of a larger group, a single direct reach toward the leader or cause you actually believe in is enough to restore clarity.
- Trust genuine, quiet affinities toward people or causes you belong with rather than forcing an arbitrary choice — sincerity matters more than the scale of what you offer.
- If you find yourself excluded from an already-formed group, seek a connection through someone closer to its center rather than staying isolated — some initial awkwardness is an acceptable cost.
- Do the unifying work without claiming credit for yourself; unselfish effort on behalf of a larger cause is what produces lasting, blameless success.
- When people gather around you because of your position rather than genuine trust, address it with patient, consistent integrity over time, not with force or demands — mistrust dissolves through demonstrated character.
