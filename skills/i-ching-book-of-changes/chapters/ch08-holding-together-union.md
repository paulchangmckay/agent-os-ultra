# Hexagram 8: Pi / Holding Together [Union]

## Core Idea
Waters on the earth flow together wherever they can, just as people gather around a single strong, sincere center — union that works depends entirely on whether that center is truly equal to the responsibility of holding others together.

## Trigrams
- Upper: K'an (Water)
- Lower: K'un (Earth)

## The Judgment
> HOLDING TOGETHER brings good fortune. Inquire of the oracle once again whether you possess sublimity, constancy, and perseverance; then there is no blame. Those who are uncertain gradually join. Whoever comes too late meets with misfortune.

**Meaning**: Becoming the center that others unite around is a serious responsibility requiring real greatness of spirit — genuine union naturally draws in the hesitant over time, but those who delay too long lose their place.

## The Image
> On the earth is water: the image of HOLDING TOGETHER. Thus the kings of antiquity bestowed the different states as fiefs and cultivated friendly relations with the feudal lords.

**Meaning**: Just as water penetrates and unites with the earth it rests on, a healthy social order distributes responsibility from the center outward while ensuring every member feels genuinely bound to the whole.

## The Lines
- **Six at the beginning**: "Hold to him in truth and loyalty; this is without blame. Truth, like a full earthen bowl: thus in the end good fortune comes from without." — Sincerity that comes from real inner substance, not clever presentation, eventually draws good fortune from outside sources.
- **Six in the second place**: "Hold to him inwardly. Perseverance brings good fortune." — Responding faithfully to a genuine inner call to serve, rather than chasing association out of ambition, preserves one's dignity.
- **Six in the third place**: "You hold together with the wrong people." — Maintaining only sociability, never real intimacy, with people outside your own sphere protects your ability to unite later with those who truly belong with you.
- **Six in the fourth place**: "Hold to him outwardly also. Perseverance brings good fortune." — Once a relationship with a worthy center is well established, showing loyalty openly is appropriate, provided one stays constant.
- **Nine in the fifth place**: "Manifestation of holding together. In the hunt the king uses beaters on three sides only and foregoes game that runs off in front. The citizens need no warning. Good fortune." — A true center of union invites no one and flatters no one; those meant to join come of their own accord, while those who resist are freely let go.
- **Six at the top**: "He finds no head for holding together. Misfortune." — Missing the right moment to commit fully to union, and hesitating instead, leads to a bad end with no way to recover it.

## Confucian Commentary Highlights
- The nine in the fifth place is the sole yang line and the hexagram's ruler: a strong, central, correct prince around whom every yielding line holds together in mutual support — the name of the hexagram is derived directly from this structure.
- The T'uan Chuan notes this hexagram is the structural inverse of the preceding one (The Army): there the center of authority sits in the second line as a general serving a ruler above; here it sits in the fifth line as the ruler himself.
- The commentary distinguishes Pi from the similarly structured Hexagram 45 (Ts'ui, Gathering Together): both share K'un below, but Pi has K'an (water) above where Ts'ui has Tui (the lake) — and where "sublimity, constancy, and perseverance" apply to the whole hexagram in Pi, they apply only to the ruling fifth line in Ts'ui.
- At the top line, the commentary contrasts "no head" here with the same phrase's favorable use in Ch'ien (The Creative): there, having no head signals humility in a hexagram of pure strength; here, in a yielding line with nothing to steady it, "no head" is simply directionless and unfortunate.

## Worked Example
Take the nine in the fifth place: "In the hunt the king uses beaters on three sides only and foregoes game that runs off in front." The commentary reads this as the essence of genuine union: the ruler drives game from three directions but deliberately leaves one side open, taking only the animals that, in effect, choose to come to him. Applied to human relationships, this means the true center of a fellowship never has to court, flatter, or coerce followers — cultivating real strength and integrity in oneself is what draws the right people, while anyone who resists is simply let go without a fight.

## Key Takeaways
- Before positioning yourself as the center others should rally around, honestly assess whether you have the substance to carry that responsibility.
- Build sincerity from real inner conviction rather than persuasive presentation — the commentary's image is a full bowl, valued for its contents, not its shape.
- Keep purely sociable, non-intimate relations with people outside your natural circle so you stay free to commit fully when you find where you truly belong.
- Once a bond with a worthy leader or partner is genuinely established, show your loyalty openly rather than holding back out of caution.
- Attract people by cultivating your own integrity rather than by inviting, pursuing, or flattering them — real union should never need to be forced.
- Don't let hesitation about committing to a union run past its natural window — waiting too long to commit fully can foreclose the good outcome entirely.
