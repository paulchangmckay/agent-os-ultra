# Hexagram 55: Fêng / Abundance [Fullness]

## Core Idea
A peak of greatness and prosperity — achievable only briefly, by a leader whose clarity and vigorous movement together create abundance, and who must stay clear-eyed that all fullness eventually wanes.

## Trigrams
- Upper: Chên (Thunder, the Arousing)
- Lower: Li (Fire, Clarity)

## The Judgment
> ABUNDANCE has success. The king attains abundance. Be not sad. Be like the sun at midday.

**Meaning**: Only a true leader can bring about a time of great abundance, and though such peaks inevitably decline, the person at the center of it must stay free of sorrow and radiate like the midday sun rather than dwell on the coming decline.

## The Image
> Both thunder and lightning come: The image of ABUNDANCE. Thus the superior man decides lawsuits And carries out punishments.

**Meaning**: Clarity (to investigate facts precisely) paired with decisive force (to enforce judgments strictly) is what abundance requires — here the two combine to actually carry out justice, not merely establish it.

## The Lines
- **Nine at the beginning**: "When a man meets his destined ruler, They can be together ten days, And it is not a mistake. Going meets with recognition." — A kindred, complementary partnership can be pursued for a full natural cycle without fault — but know when to stop; overstaying the moment turns beneficial into harmful.
- **Six in the second place**: "The curtain is of such fullness That the polestars can be seen at noon. Through going one meets with mistrust and hate. If one rouses him through truth, Good fortune comes." — When intrigue eclipses the ruler's clarity and direct approach meets only suspicion, hold to inner truthfulness; its quiet power eventually breaks through.
- **Nine in the third place**: "The underbrush is of such abundance That the small stars can be seen at noon. He breaks his right arm. No blame." — When obstruction is total and even the capable are sidelined, recognize that nothing can be accomplished now — restraint here is not fault, it's realism.
- **Nine in the fourth place**: "The curtain is of such fullness That the polestars can be seen at noon. He meets his ruler, who is of like kind. Good fortune." — As the darkness begins to lift, finding a kindred partner to complement your strength with wisdom (or wisdom with energy) restores good fortune.
- **Six in the fifth place**: "Lines are coming, Blessing and fame draw near. Good fortune." — A modest ruler who stays open to counsel draws capable people to himself, and blessing follows naturally.
- **Six at the top**: "His house is in a state of abundance. He screens off his family. He peers through the gate And no longer perceives anyone. For three years he sees nothing. Misfortune." — Arrogant insistence on dominating one's own household isolates a person completely; the very abundance sought becomes total isolation.

## Confucian Commentary Highlights
- The T'uan Chuan states plainly the universal law behind the Judgment's caution: "when the sun stands at midday, it begins to set; when the moon is full, it begins to wane... How much truer is this of men, or of spirits and gods!" — abundance is inherently self-limiting.
- Book III specifies the one safeguard against abundance collapsing into its opposite: "spiritual expansion" — sharing the abundance with ever-larger groups rather than hoarding or restricting it, since limitation invites bitter retribution.
- The hexagram is explicitly linked to Shih Ho, Biting Through (21): that hexagram lays down laws, this one enforces and carries them out — the same trigram pairing (clarity plus shock) applied to a later stage.
- Line placement matters more here through congruity (similarity of nature) than through the usual correspondence between paired lines — the text notes this is why the nine at the beginning pairs meaningfully with the nine in the fourth, and the six in the second with the six in the fifth, both matched pairs of like kind.

## Worked Example
Consider the six in the fifth place: "Lines are coming, Blessing and fame draw near. Good fortune." Book III identifies this as the ruling line of the whole hexagram — yielding and central, representing the king himself, whose modesty is precisely what draws capable, clarifying counsel toward him ("the light, clear force just approaching"). In practice: a leader at the height of success who wants that abundance to be sustainable, rather than collapse into the isolation of the top line, should actively invite input and share credit — modesty at the peak is not weakness, it's the mechanism that keeps the peak from becoming a lonely dead end.

## Key Takeaways
- Treat a season of peak success as inherently temporary; plan for its natural decline rather than being blindsided by it.
- When obstruction and intrigue are total, recognize that action is pointless for now — waiting blamelessly is the correct move, not a failure.
- Pair complementary strengths (energy with wisdom, wisdom with energy) rather than trying to do everything with one quality alone.
- Sustain abundance by sharing it broadly; hoarding success or authority is what curdles it into isolation.
- Stay especially wary of the temptation, at the height of power, to wall yourself off from others — that isolation is self-inflicted, not imposed.

## Connects To
