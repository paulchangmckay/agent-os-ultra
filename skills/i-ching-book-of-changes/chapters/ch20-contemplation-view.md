# Hexagram 20: Kuan / Contemplation (View)

## Core Idea
Contemplation carries a double meaning — contemplating and being seen as an example — picturing a ruler who studies the laws of heaven and the ways of the people, and through inner concentration and good government becomes a model that transforms others without their realizing how.

## Trigrams
- Upper: Sun, wind
- Lower: K'un, earth

## The Judgment
> CONTEMPLATION. The ablution has been made, but not yet the offering. Full of trust they look up to him.

**Meaning**: The moment of deepest inner concentration, before outward ritual is completed, is when sincere inner piety has its most transforming and awe-inspiring effect on witnesses — genuine inner power influences others silently.

## The Image
> The wind blows over the earth: the image of CONTEMPLATION. Thus the kings of old visited the regions of the world, contemplated the people, and gave them instruction.

**Meaning**: As wind travels everywhere bending the grass, a superior person surveys the real sentiments of the people so as not to be deceived, and in turn influences them profoundly by the impact of his own character.

## The Lines
- **Six at the beginning**: "Boylike contemplation. For an inferior man, no blame. For a superior man, humiliation." — Viewing influence from a distance without real comprehension is harmless for ordinary people but disgraceful for one who should understand things as a connected whole.
- **Six in the second place**: "Contemplation through the crack of the door. Furthering for the perseverance of a woman." — A narrow, self-referential outlook suits a housewife's limited sphere but is harmful for anyone who must act in public affairs.
- **Six in the third place**: "Contemplation of my life decides the choice between advance and retreat." — At the point of transition, self-contemplation replaces naive egotism; judge your path by the actual effects your life produces, not by private brooding.
- **Six in the fourth place**: "Contemplation of the light of the kingdom. It furthers one to exert influence as the guest of a king." — Someone who understands what makes a kingdom flourish should be given an honored, independent position of influence, not used as a mere tool.
- **Nine in the fifth place**: "Contemplation of my life. The superior man is without blame." — A leader others look up to must constantly examine the actual effects of his influence on others; only good effects bring the assurance of being free of mistakes.
- **Nine at the top**: "Contemplation of his life. The superior man is without blame." — At the furthest remove from ego, a sage who stands outside worldly affairs contemplates the laws of life itself, finding freedom from blame the highest good.

## Confucian Commentary Highlights
- The hexagram's rulers are the nine in the fifth place and the nine at the top; the Commentary's "a great view is above" refers to these two lines, with the fifth line — central and correct — being what "the world views."
- The nuclear trigram Kên (mountain, also meaning gates, palaces, hands, and pausing) appears twice, intermeshed, giving rise to the ritual imagery: the temple of ancestors, the washing of hands (Kên as hand, Sun as pure), and the paused, not-yet-completed sacrifice that the Judgment describes.
- The Commentary explains that the holy man, having grasped the immutable laws of heaven (like the unfailing succession of the four seasons), teaches from that knowledge, and the whole world submits to him because his predictions prove reliable.
- On the top line, the commentary notes "the will is not yet pacified" — even at the furthest remove from personal concerns, this ruler still looks down on the fifth line's affairs, showing he has not entirely forgotten the world.

## Worked Example
Take the nine in the fifth place: "Contemplation of my life. The superior man is without blame." The commentary clarifies this line as "contemplation of the people" — the ruler in the position of authority examines himself specifically by looking at the influence he has exerted on others. This sharpens the Judgment's picture of hidden, transformative inner power: it isn't self-satisfaction that confirms a leader is on the right path, but the observable good effect of his actions on those who look up to him. Self-examination here means checking outcomes, not indulging introspection for its own sake.

## Key Takeaways
- Judge your own direction by the actual effects you produce in the world, not by private self-assessment alone.
- Don't settle for a shallow, distant view of the forces around you — understand them as a connected whole.
- If you understand what makes something flourish, seek a position where you can act with real, honored independence rather than being merely used.
- A leader's silent, personal integrity has more transformative power over others than direct persuasion.
- Recognize when it's time to step fully outside personal and worldly concerns to contemplate broader, lasting laws.
