# Hexagram 17: Sui / Following

## Core Idea
Following describes how to win genuine, willing adherents: by adapting to the demands of the time and by the strong learning to place itself under the yielding, not through force, cunning, or faction.

## Trigrams
- Upper: Tui, the Joyous (lake)
- Lower: Chên, the Arousing (thunder/movement)

## The Judgment
> FOLLOWING has supreme success. Perseverance furthers. No blame.

**Meaning**: To gain a following, one must first know how to serve and adapt; a following won by force or conspiracy breeds resistance, so success here depends on persevering in what is right.

## The Image
> Thunder in the middle of the lake: the image of FOLLOWING. Thus the superior man at nightfall goes indoors for rest and recuperation.

**Meaning**: Just as thunder rests within the lake in its season, the superior man alternates tireless activity with real rest, never wearing himself out through mistaken resistance to the times.

## The Lines
- **Nine at the beginning**: "The standard is changing. Perseverance brings good fortune. To go out of the door in company produces deeds." — Stay accessible to others' views but firm in principle, and mix freely with all sorts of people rather than only the like-minded.
- **Six in the second place**: "If one clings to the little boy, one loses the strong man." — Choosing companions is exclusive: throwing in with unworthy company costs you the connection to those who could truly further you.
- **Six in the third place**: "If one clings to the strong man, one loses the little boy. Through following one finds what one seeks. It furthers one to remain persevering." — Choosing the superior connection means consciously parting with the inferior one, and holding firm to what you actually seek.
- **Nine in the fourth place**: "Following creates success. Perseverance brings misfortune. To go one's way with sincerity brings clarity. How could there be blame in this?" — A following built on flatterers seeking advantage is a trap; only freedom from ego and genuine conviction lets you see through them and stay blameless.
- **Nine in the fifth place**: "Sincere in the good. Good fortune." — Everyone needs something worth following as a lodestar; sincere devotion to the good strengthens the one who holds it.
- **Six at the top**: "He meets with firm allegiance and is still further bound. The king introduces him to the Western Mountain." — Even a sage who has withdrawn from the world can be drawn back by a follower's genuine understanding, forming a lasting bond.

## Confucian Commentary Highlights
- The hexagram's rulers are the nine at the beginning and the nine in the fifth place: both are strong lines placed under weak ones, embodying the core principle that the firm willingly subordinates itself to the yielding in order to be followed.
- Chên (movement) below and Tui (joyousness) above means people readily join a movement associated with joy — structurally, followers come easily when leadership is both active and glad.
- The image's placement of Chên in the east and Tui in the west marks the interval as night, tying the hexagram's "rest and recuperation" counsel to the natural cycle of the seasons (thunder at rest in the lake between the eighth and second months).
- Each line's fortune turns on correct placement: the nine in the fifth is auspicious because it is both correct and central, safeguarding the ruler against currying favor from those below.

## Worked Example
Take the nine in the fourth place: "Following creates success. Perseverance brings misfortune. To go one's way with sincerity brings clarity." This line is a minister who succeeds in attracting followers simply by proximity to the true ruler (the fifth line) — but because his own position is not fully correct, that success is double-edged and can draw misfortune if he comes to depend on it. The remedy the line offers is sincerity: acting from conviction rather than clinging to the following itself. This sharpens the Judgment's overall warning that a following obtained without the right inner footing, however easily it comes, is not stable.

## Key Takeaways
- Use adaptation, not force, when you need others to follow your lead — resistance follows coercion.
- Alternate genuine exertion with real rest; don't mistake constant motion for effectiveness.
- Audit who you're keeping company with — you cannot hold both worthy and unworthy connections at once.
- When you attract followers easily, check whether your own position is sound before trusting that success.
- Keep one guiding conviction ("something to follow") rather than drifting with momentary opinion.
