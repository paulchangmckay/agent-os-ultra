# Hexagram 19: Lin / Approach

## Core Idea
Approach describes a time of joyous, hopeful ascent — like spring returning — when the strong grow from below and the high approach the low with tolerance and teaching, but the propitious season is temporary and must be used with foresight.

## Trigrams
- Upper: K'un, earth
- Lower: Tui, the lake

## The Judgment
> APPROACH has supreme success. Perseverance furthers. When the eighth month comes, there will be misfortune.

**Meaning**: A time of joyous progress calls for determined action while it lasts, but since every rise gives way to retreat in time, foresight now prevents being caught off guard by the coming reversal.

## The Image
> The earth above the lake: the image of APPROACH. Thus the superior man is inexhaustible in his will to teach, and without limits in his tolerance and protection of the people.

**Meaning**: As the lake's depth is inexhaustible and the earth boundlessly sustains all things, the sage is tireless in teaching and all-inclusive in caring for people, excluding no one.

## The Lines
- **Nine at the beginning**: "Joint approach. Perseverance brings good fortune." — When the good starts to prevail and attracts capable people, join the upward trend but hold to what is right rather than being swept along by momentum.
- **Nine in the second place**: "Joint approach. Good fortune. Everything furthers." — With inner strength and consistency that need no prompting, one need not fear the transience of fortune; everything serves to further, and one can meet whatever comes swiftly and honestly.
- **Six in the third place**: "Comfortable approach. Nothing that would further. If one is induced to grieve over it, one becomes free of blame." — Power and influence bring the risk of careless ease with others, but regretting and correcting that attitude frees one of fault.
- **Six in the fourth place**: "Complete approach. No blame." — The open-minded approach of someone in high rank drawing a capable person into their circle, regardless of class prejudice, is entirely favorable.
- **Six in the fifth place**: "Wise approach. This is right for a great prince. Good fortune." — True wisdom in leadership lies in selecting able people and then giving them a free hand without interference.
- **Six at the top**: "Greathearted approach. Good fortune. No blame." — A sage who has withdrawn from the world may still return to teach and help others; this generous self-humbling brings great fortune and carries no blame.

## Confucian Commentary Highlights
- The rulers of the hexagram are the nine at the beginning and the nine in the second place; the Commentary on the Decision states "the firm penetrates and grows" — the two rising yang lines drive the whole structure's character.
- Structurally, Tui (below) moves upward while K'un (above) sinks downward, so the two trigrams move toward each other; the nuclear trigrams (Chên rising, K'un sinking) reinforce the same convergent motion, making this hexagram's organization "altogether favorable."
- The Judgment's ominous "eighth month" is explained numerically: the next hexagram, Kuan (Contemplation), in which the strong lines' retreat mirrors this hexagram's advance, falls exactly eight months later in the yearly cycle — the warning is built into the calendar itself.
- The Commentary notes that foreknowledge of fate's coming reversal, acted on early via the nuclear trigram Chên's movement, is strong enough to counteract that reversal before it sets in — approach's opportunity includes the chance to prepare for its own ending.

## Worked Example
Take the nine in the second place: "Joint approach. Good fortune. Everything furthers." The Commentary adds, "one need not yield to fate" — even though the joint ascent of the two strong lines is grounded in the natural cycle that will eventually bring regression, initiating the right movement in time is strong enough to counteract that eventual decline. This deepens the Judgment's warning about the eighth month: it isn't a call to fatalism about the coming reversal, but a reminder that timely, well-grounded action can blunt fate's downturn before it arrives.

## Key Takeaways
- Use a favorable season of rising influence energetically, but don't mistake it for permanent — plan for its eventual reversal now.
- When drawn upward by good momentum, keep your own conduct correct rather than just riding the current.
- If comfort and influence tempt you toward carelessness with others, catch and regret it early to stay free of fault.
- As a leader, the wise move is choosing capable people and then leaving them room to work, not micromanaging them.
- Recognize that even withdrawal from the world can rightly be interrupted by a genuine call to teach and help.
