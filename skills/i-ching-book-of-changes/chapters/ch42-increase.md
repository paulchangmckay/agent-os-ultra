# Hexagram 42: I / Increase

## Core Idea
Sacrifice by those above for the benefit of those below is out-and-out increase — the very principle that true rule means true service — and it fills people with a devotion that makes even great undertakings succeed.

## Trigrams
- Upper: Sun (Wind, Gentleness, Penetration)
- Lower: Chên (Thunder, the Arousing, Movement)

## The Judgment
> INCREASE. It furthers one to undertake something. It furthers one to cross the great water.

**Meaning**: When leaders sacrifice for the benefit of the people, gratitude and devotion make even difficult and dangerous undertakings succeed — but this time of flowering does not last forever and must be used while it does.

## The Image
> Wind and thunder: the image of INCREASE. Thus the superior man: if he sees good, he imitates it; if he has faults, he rids himself of them.

**Meaning**: Just as wind and thunder reinforce and strengthen each other, a person increases themselves by imitating good wherever they find it and ridding themselves of their own faults — this ethical self-correction is the most important form of increase.

## The Lines
- **Nine at the beginning**: "It furthers one to accomplish great deeds. Supreme good fortune. No blame." — Great help received from above, used selflessly to achieve something otherwise beyond one's reach, produces great good fortune and no reproach.
- **Six in the second place**: "Someone does indeed increase him; ten pairs of tortoises cannot oppose it. Constant perseverance brings good fortune. The king presents him before God. Good fortune." — Real increase comes from cultivating receptivity to the good within oneself, arriving with the inevitability of natural law, provided unexpected fortune doesn't make one heedless.
- **Six in the third place**: "One is enriched through unfortunate events. No blame, if you are sincere and walk in the middle, and report with a seal to the prince." — In a time of increase, even ordinarily unfortunate events can turn to advantage for those who act in harmony with truth and sincerity.
- **Six in the fourth place**: "If you walk in the middle and report to the prince, he will follow. It furthers one to be used in the removal of the capital." — A disinterested mediator between leader and followers, one who withholds nothing of the benefit meant to reach the people, is especially valuable for great, decisive undertakings.
- **Nine in the fifth place**: "If in truth you have a kind heart, ask not. Supreme good fortune. Truly, kindness will be recognized as your virtue." — True kindness does not calculate merit or gratitude but acts from inner necessity, and such a heart finds itself recognized and rewarded.
- **Nine at the top**: "He brings increase to no one. Indeed, someone even strikes him. He does not keep his heart constantly steady. Misfortune." — Neglecting the duty of those in high place to benefit those below leaves one isolated and vulnerable, inviting attack through an attitude out of harmony with the time.

## Confucian Commentary Highlights
- The Commentary on the Decision opens with a direct cosmic parallel: "Heaven dispenses, earth brings forth: thereby things increase in all directions" — human increase through leaders sacrificing for followers is framed as mirroring this natural process exactly.
- The hexagram is explicitly derived from Hexagram 12 (P'i, Standstill) by the transference of its lowest upper-trigram line down to the bottom of the lower trigram — a structural sibling to how Hexagram 41 (Decrease) derives from Hexagram 11 (Peace).
- The text notes the hexagram is the structural inverse of Hexagram 41 (Decrease), and that its second line's text corresponds directly to the fifth line's text in Decrease — a deliberate mirroring the commentary calls out explicitly.
- Confucius's added remark on the top line stresses preparation before action: "The superior man sets his person at rest before he moves; he composes his mind before he speaks; he makes his relations firm before he asks for something" — extending the bare line's warning about isolation into a fuller teaching on the sequence of trust-building.

## Worked Example
Consider the nine in the fifth place: "If in truth you have a kind heart, ask not. Supreme good fortune. Truly, kindness will be recognized as your virtue." A person in a position to help others — a manager, a mentor, someone with resources to share — often feels a pull to make sure their generosity is noticed or reciprocated. This line counsels against that calculation entirely: kindness that acts from genuine inner necessity, without asking after credit, is the kind that produces supreme good fortune, because it will be recognized on its own regardless. It sharpens the Judgment's larger claim that increase flowing from above to below produces boundless devotion — this line locates the exact quality of heart, unasked and uncounted generosity, that makes such increase real rather than transactional.

## Key Takeaways
- Use any unexpected strength or help you receive for something genuinely worthwhile, not personal convenience — selflessness is what keeps such fortune blameless.
- Cultivate goodness in yourself rather than chasing external increase directly; true increase arrives on its own as a consequence.
- Even setbacks, in a time of general flourishing, can become advantages if you meet them with sincerity and steady conduct.
- Act as an honest go-between when mediating between people with different levels of power — withhold nothing of the benefit meant to pass through you.
- Give without keeping score. Generosity that expects recognition is not the generosity this hexagram rewards — the disinterested kind is what draws lasting good fortune.
