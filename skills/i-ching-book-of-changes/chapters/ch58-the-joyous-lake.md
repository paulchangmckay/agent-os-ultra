# Hexagram 58: Tui / The Joyous, Lake

## Core Idea
Genuine joy — the kind that draws people together and sustains effort — must be grounded in inner firmness and truth, expressed outwardly as gentleness, never in ungoverned pleasure-seeking.

## Trigrams
- Upper: Tui (Lake, the Joyous)
- Lower: Tui (Lake, the Joyous)

## The Judgment
> THE JOYOUS. Success. Perseverance is favorable.

**Meaning**: Joy is infectious and brings success, but only when anchored in steadfastness — truth and strength must live in the heart while gentleness shows in dealings with others, or joy degenerates into uncontrolled mirth.

## The Image
> Lakes resting one on the other: The image of THE JOYOUS. Thus the superior man joins with his friends For discussion and practice.

**Meaning**: Just as two joined lakes replenish each other and resist drying up, learning and life stay vital through genuine exchange with congenial friends, not through solitary, one-sided self-instruction.

## The Lines
- **Nine at the beginning**: "Contented joyousness. Good fortune." — A quiet, self-contained joy that wants nothing from outside and harbors no likes or dislikes carries its own secure good fortune.
- **Nine in the second place**: "Sincere joyousness. Good fortune. Remorse disappears." — Holding firm against inferior pleasures, even amid dubious company, removes any cause for regret — others stop even offering what you clearly won't accept.
- **Six in the third place**: "Coming joyousness. Misfortune." — Joy that streams in from outside because one is empty within, rather than arising from genuine inner stability, leads to self-loss and misfortune.
- **Nine in the fourth place**: "Joyousness that is weighed is not at peace. After ridding himself of mistakes a man has joy." — Wavering between higher and lower pleasures brings no peace; only a clear decision to turn from the lower toward the higher resolves the inner conflict.
- **Nine in the fifth place**: "Sincerity toward disintegrating influences is dangerous." — Even the best of people can be approached by corrupting influences; recognizing the danger clearly is what protects against it.
- **Six at the top**: "Seductive joyousness." — An unstable, empty nature that gives itself over entirely to available pleasures has surrendered control of its own life to chance and outside influence.

## Confucian Commentary Highlights
- The T'uan Chuan locates the hexagram's real strength structurally: "the firm is in the middle, the yielding is without" — the two central strong lines (second and fifth) are what make outward gentleness genuine rather than hollow.
- Book III extends the political dimension explicitly: "when one leads the way for the people joyously, they forget their drudgery; when one confronts difficulty joyously, the people forget death" — joy grounded in firmness is presented as the best instrument of government, not mere sentiment.
- The Miscellaneous Notes note Tui's double meaning: it is both lake (which "rejoices and refreshes") and mouth (through which people manifest joy to one another) — but also carries a "cutting and destroying quality," being linked to autumn and metal, a reminder joy has an edge.
- Tui is identified as the structural inverse of Sun (57), pairing gentle penetration with joyous manifestation as two related but opposite modes of influence.

## Worked Example
Take the nine in the fourth place: "Joyousness that is weighed is not at peace. After ridding himself of mistakes a man has joy." Book III places this line between the strong ruler above (nine in the fifth) and the seductive six in the third below, which is "trying to seduce it." The line has "still not altogether attained peace," but possesses enough inner strength to choose whom to follow and sever the tempting relationship. Applied practically: when you feel torn between a higher, more demanding good and an easier, lower distraction, the discomfort of that weighing is itself normal — resolution comes only from deciding decisively which to follow, not from trying to hold both at once.

## Key Takeaways
- Ground your enjoyment in something inwardly stable; joy that depends entirely on external stimulation is fragile and can turn to misfortune.
- Holding firm against low-quality distractions, even around people who indulge in them, eventually stops those temptations from even being offered to you.
- When torn between competing pleasures or paths, recognize that indecision itself is the discomfort — a clear choice for the higher path is what restores peace.
- Stay alert to corrupting influences even at your strongest; recognizing danger clearly is what actually protects you from it.
- Seek real, mutual exchange with genuine companions rather than isolated self-instruction — like joined lakes, shared engagement keeps understanding alive.

## Connects To
