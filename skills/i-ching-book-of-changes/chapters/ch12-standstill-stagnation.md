# Hexagram 12: P'i / Standstill [Stagnation]

## Core Idea
Heaven draws away above while earth sinks further below — the creative powers lose all relation to each other, producing a time of stagnation in which the superior man's only real work is to withdraw into his own integrity rather than compromise it.

## Trigrams
- Upper: Ch'ien (Heaven, strength)
- Lower: K'un (Earth, devotion)

## The Judgment
> STANDSTILL. Evil people do not further the perseverance of the superior man. The great departs; the small approaches.

**Meaning**: Heaven and earth are out of communion and confusion prevails; the way of inferior people is ascending while the way of superior people declines — the superior man does not abandon his principles but withdraws into seclusion rather than compromise them.

## The Image
> Heaven and earth do not unite: the image of STANDSTILL. Thus the superior man falls back upon his inner worth in order to escape the difficulties. He does not permit himself to be honored with revenue.

**Meaning**: When mistrust dominated by inferior men makes fruitful public activity impossible, the superior man refuses tempting offers to participate, hides his worth, and withdraws rather than risk being compromised.

## The Lines
- **Six at the beginning**: "When ribbon grass is pulled up, the sod comes with it. Each according to his kind. Perseverance brings good fortune and success." — Withdrawing from public life draws like-minded others along too; this deliberate retirement, not further engagement, is what brings good fortune here.
- **Six in the second place**: "They bear and endure; this means good fortune for inferior people. The standstill serves to help the great man to attain success." — Inferior people flatter those in power for their own advantage, but the great man refuses to mix into that sycophancy, safeguarding his principles by quietly enduring the standstill instead.
- **Six in the third place**: "They bear shame." — Those who have risen to power illegitimately begin to feel, even if they don't yet show it, the shame of a responsibility they cannot really carry — marking the first turn toward improvement.
- **Nine in the fourth place**: "He who acts at the command of the highest remains without blame. Those of like mind partake of the blessing." — Restoring order requires genuine authority and calling, not self-appointed initiative; the truly called person's success benefits all who share his aims.
- **Nine in the fifth place**: "Standstill is giving way. Good fortune for the great man. 'What if it should fail, what if it should fail?' In this way he ties it to a cluster of mulberry shoots." — The right person able to end the stagnation has arrived, but only continuous, anxious caution — never complacent security — makes the recovery secure.
- **Nine at the top**: "The standstill comes to an end. First standstill, then good fortune." — Stagnation does not end on its own; it takes deliberate effort by the right person to reverse it, just as maintaining peace requires continuous effort to keep it from decaying into stagnation in the first place.

## Confucian Commentary Highlights
- The six in the second place is the constituting ruler (one who "takes refuge in his virtue in order to avoid difficulties") and the nine in the fifth place is the governing ruler (one who actually "transforms standstill into peace") — two different modes of response to the same stagnant condition.
- The T'uan Chuan describes this hexagram point-for-point as the reverse of Hexagram 11 (Peace): dark within and light without, weakness within and firmness without, inferior men at the center of government and superior men pushed to the periphery — the way of the inferior man waxing while the way of the superior man wanes.
- The Sequence of the Hexagrams explains P'i's place after T'ai (Peace) with a simple principle: "things cannot remain forever united" — union inevitably gives way to its opposite in time.
- The commentary notes this hexagram shares its two nuclear trigrams (Sun, gentleness, and Kên, keeping still) with Hexagram 18 (Ku, Work on What Has Been Spoiled), where the theme of standstill likewise appears.
- Confucius's comment on the nine in the fifth place is preserved directly: "Danger arises when a man feels secure in his position... Therefore the superior man does not forget danger in his security, nor ruin when he is well established, nor confusion when his affairs are in order. In this way he gains personal safety and is able to protect the empire" — continuous vigilance, not achieved security, is what actually holds a recovery in place.

## Worked Example
Take the nine in the fifth place: "Standstill is giving way. Good fortune for the great man. 'What if it should fail, what if it should fail?'" The commentary explains that the right person to end the stagnation has now arrived and holds the correct, central position to do so — yet the line pairs this good fortune with an image of tying something to a cluster of mulberry shoots, the unusually strong new growth that sprouts when a mulberry bush is cut down. The lesson is that the moment recovery becomes possible is exactly the moment complacency becomes most dangerous; security must be actively fastened down through ongoing caution, not simply assumed once the right conditions appear.

## Key Takeaways
- When circumstances make honest, effective participation impossible, treat deliberate withdrawal as a form of success, not defeat — it protects what matters most: your integrity.
- Don't let flattery or sycophantic access to power tempt you into compromising your principles, even when going along would be personally advantageous.
- Watch for the first, private signs that people who gained power illegitimately are beginning to feel the weight of it — that quiet shame is often the earliest sign a bad situation is starting to turn.
- Only act to fix a broken situation if you have genuine standing and calling to do so; self-appointed fixes without real authority tend to fail.
- Even after the right person and the right moment for recovery arrive, treat continued anxious vigilance as necessary, not as a sign something is still wrong — security dropped too soon undoes the gain.
- Recognize that stagnation, like peace, does not resolve itself — reversing decline always requires deliberate, sustained effort from someone willing to do the work.
