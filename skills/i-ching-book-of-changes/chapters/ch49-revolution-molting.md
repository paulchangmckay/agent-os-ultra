# Hexagram 49: Ko / Revolution (Molting)

## Core Idea
A radical, necessary break with an outworn order — undertaken only when confidence is present and the timing is truly ripe, like an animal molting its coat.

## Trigrams
- Upper: Tui (the Joyous, Lake)
- Lower: Li (the Clinging, Fire)

## The Judgment
> REVOLUTION. On your own day You are believed. Supreme success, Furthering through perseverance. Remorse disappears.

**Meaning**: Deep change should be undertaken only under real necessity, by someone the people already trust, and only once the moment is ripe — done rightly, it succeeds and leaves no regret.

## The Image
> Fire in the lake: the image of REVOLUTION. Thus the superior man Sets the calendar in order And makes the seasons clear.

**Meaning**: Just as fire and water contend to produce the turning of the seasons, the superior man brings clarity and order out of change by naming and marking its regularity rather than letting it stay chaotic.

## The Lines
- **Nine at the beginning**: "Wrapped in the hide of a yellow cow." — Hold still and restrain yourself; the moment for action has not come.
- **Six in the second place**: "When one's own day comes, one may create revolution. Starting brings good fortune. No blame." — Once trust and timing align, act — this is not a mistake.
- **Nine in the third place**: "Starting brings misfortune. Perseverance brings danger. When talk of revolution has gone the rounds three times, One may commit himself, And men will believe him." — Avoid both reckless haste and excessive hesitation; act once the need has been voiced and tested repeatedly.
- **Nine in the fourth place**: "Remorse disappears. Men believe him. Changing the form of government brings good fortune." — With real authority and honest motive behind it, structural change succeeds.
- **Nine in the fifth place**: "The great man changes like a tiger. Even before he questions the oracle He is believed." — A great person's change is so clear and just that it wins spontaneous trust, no confirmation needed.
- **Six at the top**: "The superior man changes like a panther. The inferior man molts in the face. Starting brings misfortune. To remain persevering brings good fortune." — After the big change, only smaller refinements remain; pushing further invites unrest — be satisfied with what is attainable.

## Confucian Commentary Highlights
- The T'uan Chuan frames revolution as fire and water mutually subduing each other — two temperaments (the daughters Li and Tui) that cannot coexist without conflict, hence change is inevitable.
- "On your own day you are believed" is tied to the cyclic-sign system: trust is won on the day just before the actual turning point, not after.
- Legitimate revolution requires four conditions in sequence: waiting for the right moment, proceeding in a way that keeps the people's sympathy, acting from selfless motives, and answering a genuine need — the historical model given is the rulers T'ang and Wu.
- Heaven and earth themselves "revolutionize" to produce the four seasons — grounding political revolution in a cosmic, not merely social, pattern.

## Worked Example
Take the nine in the fourth place: "Remorse disappears. Men believe him. Changing the form of government brings good fortune." This line sits between the preparatory caution of the lower lines and the full authority of the great man in the fifth place — it is the moment structural change actually gets enacted. The commentary stresses that belief follows only when the change "corresponds with a higher truth," not personal ambition. Applied practically: this is the line for someone with real standing who is about to formalize a change already validated by repeated, well-founded demand — the test is whether the motive is genuinely just, because only that earns lasting support.

## Key Takeaways
- Do not force revolutionary change until there is a demonstrated, repeated need and someone trusted enough to lead it.
- Distinguish the big, necessary change from the smaller follow-on adjustments — do not try to accomplish both at once.
- Check your motive before acting: change backed by selfish aims collapses; change answering real need endures.
- When the moment is truly ripe, decisive action does not require external validation — clarity itself carries conviction.
- After the main change lands, stop — over-refining invites the very instability the revolution was meant to end.
