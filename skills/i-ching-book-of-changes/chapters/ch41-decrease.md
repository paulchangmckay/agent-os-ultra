# Hexagram 41: Sun / Decrease

## Core Idea
Decrease of what is below to increase what is above is not inherently bad — done at the right time and with sincerity, simplicity itself becomes the source of inner strength rather than a mark of poverty.

## Trigrams
- Upper: Kên (Mountain, Keeping Still)
- Lower: Tui (the Lake, the Joyous)

## The Judgment
> DECREASE combined with sincerity brings about supreme good fortune without blame. One may be persevering in this. It furthers one to undertake something. How is this to be carried out? One may use two small bowls for the sacrifice.

**Meaning**: Decrease is not automatically negative — what matters is understanding the time and not disguising scarcity with false show; when inner sincerity is present, even the simplest offering (two small bowls) is enough, because the strength of the content compensates for the plainness of the form.

## The Image
> At the foot of the mountain, the lake: the image of DECREASE. Thus the superior man controls his anger and restrains his instincts.

**Meaning**: The lake evaporates and enriches the mountain above it, just as a person must decrease the lower drives of anger and unchecked passion to enrich the higher aspects of the self.

## The Lines
- **Nine at the beginning**: "Going quickly when one's tasks are finished is without blame. But one must reflect on how much one may decrease others." — Helping others unselfishly after finishing one's own work is good, but whoever receives that help must weigh carefully how much can be taken without real harm to the giver.
- **Nine in the second place**: "Perseverance furthers. To undertake something brings misfortune. Without decreasing oneself, one is able to bring increase to others." — Serving others well requires self-respect and consistency; throwing oneself away to please a superior diminishes one's own position without giving lasting benefit.
- **Six in the third place**: "When three people journey together, their number decreases by one. When one man journeys alone, he finds a companion." — Three together breed jealousy and one must leave; one person alone is certain to find a completing companion.
- **Six in the fourth place**: "If a man decreases his faults, it makes the other hasten to come and rejoice. No blame." — Giving up one's faults, even in humility, frees well-disposed friends from an inner pressure and draws them closer with mutual joy.
- **Six in the fifth place**: "Someone does indeed increase him. Ten pairs of tortoises cannot oppose it. Supreme good fortune." — When fate marks someone for good fortune, it arrives without fail, and no oracle can contradict it.
- **Nine at the top**: "If one is increased without depriving others, there is no blame. Perseverance brings good fortune. It furthers one to undertake something. One obtains servants but no longer has a separate home." — Increase that benefits the whole world rather than a private interest brings no decrease to anyone else; such a person's gains become a public good.

## Confucian Commentary Highlights
- The hexagram is explicitly derived from Hexagram 11 (T'ai, Peace) by the transference of its third strong line to the top — showing Decrease as a structural transformation of an existing balanced hexagram, not an independent configuration.
- The Sequence and Miscellaneous Notes together frame Decrease and its paired opposite, Increase, as "the beginning of flowering and of decline" respectively — decrease reaching its goal is said to be precisely where flowering begins.
- The Commentary on the Decision stresses timing above all: "There is a time for decreasing the firm, and a time for increasing the yielding... All that matters is that things should happen at the right time" — decrease is morally neutral, judged entirely by whether it fits the moment.
- The third line's imagery of three becoming two, and one becoming two, is tied to the origin of the trigrams Kên and Tui themselves from the interchange of Ch'ien and K'un — the line's content is presented as encoding the hexagram's own cosmological genesis.

## Worked Example
Consider the six in the fourth place: "If a man decreases his faults, it makes the other hasten to come and rejoice. No blame." Someone aware that a particular flaw — impatience, defensiveness, an old grudge — has been quietly keeping well-meaning people at a distance faces a simple but demanding choice: give up the fault, or keep the distance it creates. This line shows that the decrease required is not of resources or position but of one's own faults, and that this kind of decrease produces an increase in relationship that outweighs anything given up. It extends the Judgment's core claim — that decrease done sincerely brings supreme good fortune — into the personal register: the "sacrifice" that matters most may be internal rather than material.

## Key Takeaways
- Don't mistake scarce means for failure — sincerity and simplicity can carry as much weight as elaborate resources, if the time calls for restraint.
- Serve others without abandoning your own dignity or position; self-erasure does not create lasting value for anyone.
- If you find yourself in a group of three creating friction, expect that one relationship will have to give way — closeness concentrates naturally in pairs.
- Give up a specific personal fault deliberately, as an act of decrease, and expect it to draw good-willed people closer rather than pushing them away.
- When you gain benefit or influence, check whether it comes at someone else's cost — increase that helps everyone is safe to accept; increase that decreases others is not.
