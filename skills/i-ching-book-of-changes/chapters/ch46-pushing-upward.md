# Hexagram 46: Shêng / Pushing Upward

## Core Idea
Like a plant pushing up through the earth, ascent from lowliness to influence is possible without obstruction, but it requires sustained effort and modest adaptability rather than force — a direct, effortful vertical rise rather than an easy expansion.

## Trigrams
- Upper: K'un (Earth, the Receptive)
- Lower: Sun (Wood/Wind, Gentleness, Penetration)

## The Judgment
> PUSHING UPWARD has supreme success. One must see the great man. Fear not. Departure toward the south brings good fortune.

**Meaning**: The pushing upward of good elements meets no real obstruction and brings great success, made possible not by violence but by modesty and adaptability; one must actively seek out authoritative people and set to work, since activity itself ("the south") is what brings the good fortune.

## The Image
> Within the earth, wood grows: the image of PUSHING UPWARD. Thus the superior man of devoted character heaps up small things in order to achieve something high and great.

**Meaning**: Just as wood grows upward through the earth by bending around obstacles, without haste and without rest, the superior man of devoted character makes steady, imperceptible progress that accumulates into something great.

## The Lines
- **Six at the beginning**: "Pushing upward that meets with confidence brings great good fortune." — Rising from a low, obscure station, the ascent succeeds because of a spiritual affinity and solidarity with those above who share its confidence.
- **Nine in the second place**: "If one is sincere, it furthers one to bring even a small offering. No blame." — A strong but brusque man, paying little attention to outward form, still meets a favorable response because his uprightness of character carries more weight than polish.
- **Nine in the third place**: "One pushes upward into an empty city." — Obstructions fall away with remarkable ease; the wise course is to press forward and profit from this propitiousness without being inhibited by misgivings about how long it will last.
- **Six in the fourth place**: "The king offers him Mount Ch'i. Good fortune. No blame." — Pushing upward attains its goal in full: acquiring recognized standing among those who foster the spiritual life of a community, a significance that endures.
- **Six in the fifth place**: "Perseverance brings good fortune. One pushes upward by steps." — Advancing further and further, it is essential not to become intoxicated by success but to proceed slowly, step by step, overleaping nothing.
- **Six at the top**: "Pushing upward in darkness. It furthers one to be unremittingly persevering." — Blind advance without any capacity for retreat leads to exhaustion; only conscientious, consistent perseverance frees one from this harmful blind impulse.

## Confucian Commentary Highlights
- The Commentary on the Decision makes an unusual grammatical note: the Judgment says "one must see the great man," not the more typical "it furthers one to see the great man" — because the actual ruler of the hexagram (the yielding six in the fifth place) is not itself the great man, so the reason for success is described as "not an earthly but a transcendental one."
- The hexagram is explicitly noted as the structural inverse of Hexagram 45 (Gathering Together), and is contrasted with Hexagram 35 (Progress): pushing upward here is neither as easy nor as extensive as the sun's rising in Progress, being reinforced instead by nuclear trigrams Chên and Tui that both tend upward.
- The Image's "heaping up of small things" is tied directly to the imperceptibly gradual, invisible growth of wood within earth — steady accumulation, not sudden leaps, is presented as the literal mechanism the trigram structure encodes.
- The progression across the six lines is explicitly summarized as a staged sequence: confidence, then small sacrifices, then an easy advance into a deserted city, then admission to wider recognition — the commentary frames the whole hexagram as one continuous, cumulative ascent.

## Worked Example
Consider the six in the fifth place: "Perseverance brings good fortune. One pushes upward by steps." Someone experiencing a run of unexpected success — rapid promotion, a project gaining momentum faster than planned — faces the temptation to accelerate further, skipping steps to capitalize on the moment. This line counsels the opposite: precisely at the point of greatest success, remain sober and continue advancing step by step rather than leaping ahead. It sharpens the Judgment's counsel to "fear not" by adding a necessary caution: fearlessness in pushing upward must be paired with discipline, or the very success that fuels the ascent can become the cause of its collapse.

## Key Takeaways
- Draw confidence from genuine solidarity with those who share your direction — this connection, not force, is what makes an ascent from a low position succeed.
- Substance and sincerity matter more than polish; an upright character carries weight even without smooth outward form.
- When obstacles fall away with unexpected ease, use the opportunity fully rather than second-guessing how long it will last.
- As success builds, resist the urge to skip stages — steady, step-by-step progress is what actually reaches the goal intact.
- Recognize when advance has become blind, incapable of retreat, and correct it with disciplined, conscientious perseverance rather than continued momentum.

## Connects To
- Explicitly the structural inverse of Hexagram 45, Ts'ui / Gathering Together.
- Contrasted directly in source commentary with Hexagram 35, Chin / Progress, as a slower, more effortful form of rising.
